## Summary

A hero dies when damage or a script kill brings its health to 0. The death takes effect in the same game tick. A level can register one death callback for its area object. A callback task does not overlap an earlier task for that area. Only the active area's update advances or reaps its callback task; an inactive buffered area's task remains paused and can resume if that area becomes active again. A later eligible death can start a new callback after the previous task finishes and the active-area update reaps it. Area reset clears the callback handle without releasing the still-running task through the normal per-handle release path. Replacing that area's world buffer rewinds the owning pool, reclaiming the area object and the callback manager's task storage; the old area can no longer advance the task. Replacement also runs the world module's bound Exit and unload hooks as module-level teardown; they are not tied to the callback task. No explicit task cancellation or per-task finalizer is observed, so cleanup of external resources held by an unfinished callback remains unknown (Open question 1). The callback flag decides whether the default death is skipped. The default death puts the hero in the death state, clears its melee hit list and sets its 14 status timers to -1, and plays a cue when the partner is still alive. A hero can be left at zero health outside the death state (see Edge cases).

Death costs almost nothing. There are no lives. The only penalty is the respawn after a deadly-ground death, which takes up to 5 HP and never kills (section 8A). The engine ends the game after a death only through timer E: when the dead hero's partner is also dead or cannot take over, or when a hero dies on deadly ground that has no respawn entry, timer E starts and sets the game-over flag after more than 1.5 s (section 8A). A script call can also set the same game-state flag. The next pass of play then shows the game-over page, which waits for Cross and then leads to the card state, the countdown, attract and the title screen (section 4). One level script (module 1432) calls game over inside a scripted ending. That ending is the timeout failure of the final fight, which runs when the 180 s fight clock reaches 0.0. It is reachable in play. It is not a death, and its steps do not depend on hero health (section 4). A death never respawns a hero at a save point. A death on deadly ground that carries a respawn entry index puts the hero back at that entry record of the current world after more than 1.5 s, in state 60, with 5 HP less (never below 1) (section 8A). After any other death the partner takes over at the dead hero's spot when the swap mode allows it, or the game ends; no scene setup runs. A dead hero is brought back only by a revive, a world load, the Continue reset or a script move (section 2A). Every world-load placement takes a dead selected hero out of the death state without healing it (section 8). After a game over, Continue resumes from the last save or load made in the session (kept in memory, no card access); with none, it leads to the title flow (section 4).

The clean side can build from this spec: the hit and death rules, script kills, the per-area death callback, the partner revive, falls and kill volumes, deadly ground and its timed respawn, the hurting surface and the deep overlay kill, the game-over timer, the game-over page and its exit, and the timings.

## Where it lives on the disc

- The hit routine, the death hook, the default death, the callback gate, the revive helper, the death-state handler, timers E and F and the game-over setter are in the game executable. The 5 percent heavy-hit rule, the 1.5 s timer rule, the 0.95 clip progress test and the 0.085 deadly-ground test are instruction constants in that code, not data words. They have no disc data words to read (Open question 18).
- Level scripts are archive resources, numbered as in SPEC-0001. The kill, damage, move, revive, callback and game-over calls are in these modules: 732, 735, 864, 1089, 1090, 1091, 1098, 1122, 1174, 1176, 1217, 1220, 1253, 1371, 1381, 1394, 1428 and 1432. Module 1244 holds an entry scene on a registered level trigger that revives a dead parked hero; it is not a death callback (see Revive).
- The pass rate of 60 per second used by the timers and the game-over page is set once at start-up. It is read from the first entry (row 0, column 0) of the boot rate table at data address 0x30C598, which holds 60.0, and is written to the frame-rate word at data address 0x368FA0. The running rate is not stored on the disc.
- Entry records are 80-byte records in the world pack's type-11 chunk: the chunk header holds the entry count at +0x24 and the address of entry 0 at +0x4C (rebased at load), and entry i is at that address plus 80 x i (SPEC-0006). The same list is the locator list of MoveHero and the record list of SetHeroStartPos (SPEC-0030). 179 of the 192 world packs have entries.
- Deadly ground is a property of ground model-set entries (the entry header of SPEC-0018): kind word +0x38 = 2. On the disc there are 123 such entries in 50 packs; 79 carry a respawn entry index, every one below its pack's entry count, and 44 carry none (High). Pack 735's swamp floor (entry 39) has respawn byte 255, so any fall in the arena or on the approach stones ends in game over; its western neighbour (entry 28, respawn entry 57) exists only beyond x 3625.
- Hurting surfaces and deep overlays (section 9) are also properties of ground entries: surface word +0x40 = 2 on a non-deadly entry (23 entries in 7 packs), and the linked overlay entry at +0x0E (365 links on the disc). The 2.3 overlay limit is the data word 0x2DD43C; the 10 HP, 2.5 s and 0.2 values are code constants (High).
- The game-state record, its flags, timers, the hero objects, the save-point index, the pending flag and the session snapshot exist only at run time. Nothing about them is stored on the disc.

## Behavior

### 1. Damage and the hit routine

Taken in order when a hit arrives on a hero:

1. If the input-lock word is set, the hit does nothing: no damage, no cue, no reaction. Nothing else in this list runs. The word is the game-state box flag, set while a dialogue box or a list picker is up, so every hit on a hero is discarded while one is shown (High).
2. Hit cue and hit effect, in every state except the input lock. The hit is heavy when the hero's maximum health times 0.05 is less than the damage (single precision; the 0.05 is a code constant), and light otherwise. Light plays sound event 19 and heavy plays sound event 20. When the hero's branch word is non-zero, the pair is 43 (light) and 44 (heavy) instead. A hit effect is also started. (High for the sound ids; Medium for the branch word's meaning.)
3. If the hero is in the death state, the hit stops here: no damage, no state change, no reaction, and an active stun is not cleared. A hit on a dead hero therefore still plays the cue and the effect. (High.)
4. Reaction state: if the hit's flag argument is 0 and the hero is not busy, the hero is written to state 17 and plays animation 0xC0, or animation 0x20 when a weapon test passes and a special test fails. Otherwise no state is written, and a stop call may run instead. (Medium for the busy test and the two tests, which are not described here.)
5. Damage is applied last:
   - if the damage is less than the health, health = health - damage;
   - otherwise health = 0 and the death hook runs for this hero with gate-allowed set to 1.
   Damage equal to health is a kill.
6. After damage, if the hero is in state 25 the hero is written to state 1. This is the only state-1 write in the hit routine. (High.)
7. The health display is refreshed.

Consequences:

- Because the state 17 write comes before damage, a lethal hit whose death the callback skips leaves the hero in state 17, not in its previous state.
- The hit cue and effect of a hit on a dead hero play even though nothing else happens (step 3).

### 2. Death hook

The death hook takes two inputs: the hero, and gate-allowed (1 when the death comes from damage, 0 when it comes from a script kill). It runs these steps in order:

1. If the hero is already in the death state, the gate is not consulted. Go to step 4.
2. Gate. If gate-allowed is 1 and the hero's area object has a registered death callback, the gate starts that callback as a background task when no task from that callback is still running (see Death callbacks). The gate's result is the callback's flag. If there is no callback, the result is 0. If gate-allowed is 0, the gate is not consulted and the result is 0.
3. If the result is 1, stop. The hero is not put in the death state, its hit list and status timers are kept, and no death cue plays.
4. Default death:
   - a. The hero enters the death state (16). When the hero was in another state before, the death voice plays: sound event 21 for Tor or 45 for Roberta, with a positional cue. A repeated death of a hero already in state 16 does not play it again (High).
   - b. The melee hit list is cleared.
   - c. All 14 status timers are set to -1 (the inactive value).
   - d. Death cue test: if at least one hero is not in the death state after step a, sound event 3 plays (the partner-alive cue). If both heroes are in the death state, no death cue plays.

Consequences:

- A death from damage (gate-allowed 1) runs the gate. A death from a script kill (gate-allowed 0) skips it, so a callback never sees that death.
- KillCurrentHero (script kill of the active hero) runs step 4 at once. Health is not changed.
- The script KillBothHeroes call runs the death hook for hero 0 first and then hero 1, both with gate-allowed 0. Health is not changed. The death cue plays once: on the first call the other hero is alive; on the second call both are in the death state. The separate deadly-ground motion helper is described in section 8A and allows the callback gate for both heroes.
- The death cue is the only cue added by death. The hit cue from Behavior 1 still plays on the killing hit.
- High: a direct kill of a hero already in the death state runs step 4 again in full (state 16 is written, the hit list and the status timers are cleared, and the death cue test runs). A hit never reaches this case, because a hit on a hero in the death state stops at Behavior 1, step 3. The paths that do reach it are: script kills, plain deducts (the DeductHitPoints host call and the state-60 penalty of section 8A, which skip the hit routine; any deduct on a dead hero at 0 HP runs the death hook with the gate allowed), the per-tick deadly-ground and deep overlay kills (section 8A), and the Continue hero reset (section 4).

### 2A. What happens when a hero dies: the whole flow

Confidence: High for the order of the steps and for each branch; Medium-High for the visible timings (clip length, the swap task's waits); Medium for the picture of the arrival pose. The sections named in each step hold the details and the test vectors.

Dead means state 16. Every dead test in the game reads the action state, not the health: the switch refusal, the both-dead flag of the callback gate, the revive helper, the game-over tests of modules 1122, 1217 and 1428, and the HUD. A hero at 0 HP in any other state (state 17 after a callback skip, state 1 after a scripted move) counts as alive for all of them, and a hero at 0 HP does not regenerate (SPEC-0036, Regeneration).

1. Cause. Damage that brings health to 0 (Behavior 1), a plain deduct (section 6), the deadly-ground and deep-overlay kills (sections 8A and 9), a script kill (section 6) or, outside any death, the scripted ending of section 4. Only the damage routes consult the death callback; a script kill goes straight to step 3.
2. Callback gate (section 5). It runs only when the hero's area has a registered callback. On every call it first stores the dying hero's id and the both-dead flag in the area's death-info record, even when it starts nothing. It starts the callback task when the area's task handle is empty and returns the registered flag. Flag 1: the default death is skipped and the hero stays at 0 HP in the state the hit left it (17). Flag 0: the default death runs as well. The disc registers five callbacks, each from a module's Init or from a thread that Init starts: modules 735, 1176 and 1371 (flag 1), and modules 1098 and 1394 (flag 0). No other module contains a call to it, including routines reached only through constructor lists or callback tables.
3. Default death (section 2). State 16 with the death voice, the hit list and the 14 status timers cleared, and sound event 3 when the partner is not in state 16.
4. Death-state handler (section 8A), on every tick while the selected hero is in state 16. The branch depends on the ground under the hero:
   - Ordinary ground: it plays clip 34 (17 frames, one shot, a quarter of a frame per tick), so the progress passes 0.95 about 63 ticks after the death. Then: partner in state 16, game over (step 6). Otherwise a switch request to the partner, which the swap mode can refuse (table below); a refusal is also game over.
   - Deadly ground with a respawn entry index: step 7.
   - Deadly ground without an index: the motion update has already killed both heroes; game over (step 6).
5. Take-over. An accepted request runs the hero swap (SPEC-0053, section 3.4): the white flash and sound, the partner put at the dead hero's position and facing, selected, in state 1 and with the health it has, the HUD bars shown for 4 s, and a hold of 41 ticks. The dead hero stays parked in state 16 and is not updated. No timer starts and no page appears. The player goes on from the dead hero's spot.
6. Game over (section 4). Timer E sets flag E on its 91st addition; the world freezes under the game-over page; Cross, newly pressed on page pass 122 or later, leaves it. With a save or load made earlier in the session, Continue re-applies the session snapshot from memory: health, magic and statuses of both heroes come from the slot, the selected hero is put at the saved position and facing in state 59, the other hero is in state 1 (a hero with health below 1 gets a default death instead), the areas reload and every module starts fresh. With none pending, the world is torn down and the front end goes through the card state, the logo page and the attract screen to the title screen; a new game or a load starts from the new-game or the saved values (SPEC-0025).
7. Deadly-ground respawn (section 8A). Timer F puts the same hero at the entry record named by the ground entry on its 91st addition, in state 60 (clip 137), with 5 HP less (never below 1).
8. The parked dead hero. A hero left in state 16 stays dead through area changes and keeps its health. It comes back only through: the Potion of Revival (item 50) and the NeedHeroAlive calls, both gated on swap mode 2, which put the partner of the selected hero in state 1 at full health (modules 1244 and 1394 make the calls, section 7); the boss refill call (SPEC-0030, row 165), which first revives a downed parked hero for most encounters; a save load or Continue, which set every hero state to 1 or 59 and take the health from the slot (a hero saved at 0 HP loads in state 1 at 0 HP); and a script move with control option 1 of the selected hero (state 1, health unchanged). The act-start heals (section 8, item 1) raise health but never change a state, so a hero in state 16 stays dead after one.
9. No scene setup, no checkpoint, no penalty. A death never calls the act-start setup or any other area setup, never puts a hero at a save point (sections 3 and 8), and takes no money or items. The only heals reachable after a death are the item and petpet heals, the act-start heals and the boss refill.

Module 1098 arrival (Medium-High). The module's callback has flag 0, so the default death runs first, and its task asks for the reload on the pass of the death or the next. The reload and the death handler then run side by side: the world update keeps running while the load is pending. The load begins about 7 passes after the request (a 6-frame fade-out and the request timer, SPEC-0026), then reads the pack, which is 3,857 sectors (7,899,136 bytes); the arrival placement runs when the load completes. The death handler chooses at about pass 63 after the death (the clip passes 0.95). The death mode word is cleared at the start of every handler tick and is 1 only after a game-over choice, so the state the arrival writes depends on whether the choice has been made. The read alone would have to sustain 8.5 million bytes per second to finish before pass 63, which the console's DVD drive does not do (Medium), so the arrival normally comes after the choice. Two cases follow. Switch refused (the selected hero is the one the swap mode locks to, or the partner is dead): death mode 1, the arrival writes state 59, and timer E, which started at the choice, is not stopped by any load, so the game-over page still follows 91 passes after the choice: the reload does not save the player. Partner takes over (swap mode 2 with the partner alive): the death mode stays 0, the arrival puts the selected hero at entry record 0 in state 1, and the dead hero stays parked in state 16 (a load places only the selected hero). If the pack were read before pass 63 the dead hero would arrive in state 1 at 0 HP, count as alive for every dead test, and never run the handler. Test vectors (synthetic): Tor selected in swap mode 0, killed on pass 100: the handler chooses on about pass 163, timer E acts on pass 254, an arrival on pass 210 writes state 59 and the page appears on pass 254. Tor killed on pass 100 in swap mode 2 with Roberta alive: Roberta takes over at about pass 163, an arrival on pass 210 writes state 1, and no game over follows.

The arrival pose of state 59 (clip 175, hero action id 0xAF). It is a looping clip of 30 keys with no root travel. The pelvis bone is held 0.92 (Tor) or 0.95 (Roberta) model units above its height in the standing idle clip (Tor 2.09 against 1.17, Roberta 2.14 against 1.19), and the other bones lie up to about 66 degrees (Tor) or 45 degrees (Roberta) from the standing idle pose, moving at most about 26 degrees during the loop. The hero therefore hovers just above the ground with bent limbs, with a one-shot effect at the hero, until the clip's progress reaches 0.99 (pass 119 counting the request pass as 1); then it drops to state 1 with sound event 0. The state reads no stick or button, and the chord's switch-ready gate accepts only states 1 and 20, so neither steering nor the player's hero switch is possible while it lasts. High for the clip data and the timing; Medium for the picture.

### 3. No lives, no penalty

- No lives counter exists. The death path takes no money, no items and no other resources. The only death penalty is health: the respawn after a deadly-ground death (state 60) deducts up to 5 HP and never kills (section 8A). (High for the death path; Medium for the whole game.) The only continue is the game-over page's Continue (section 4), which resumes from the last save or load of the session.

### 4. Game over

- SetGameOver(n) sets flag E. The argument is ignored by the engine. Scripts pass 0 (modules 1122 and 1428), 2 (module 1217) and 1 (module 1432: the timeout failure of the final fight, a scripted ending that is not a death).
- Timer E: flag E is also set when timer E's accumulated time first passes 1.5 s, that is on the first pass where it is more than 90 ticks. Timer E plays sound event 5 at the same time. This is the 91st addition of dt (section 8A; High). Timer E is started by the death-state handler (section 8A), and only when no game over is pending and timer E is idle. Timer E keeps counting while the game is paused: the game-state timers run on every pass, and the pause opener does not test timer E. So pausing during the 1.5 s delay does not stop the game over; flag E can be set under the pause, and the game-over page follows the first in-play pass after the resume (Medium-High).
- Health 0 does not set flag E directly. A death reaches game over in two ways: through the death-state handler, which starts timer E when the dying hero's partner is also dead, when the switch to the partner is refused, or when the hero died on deadly ground with no respawn entry (section 8A); or through a script call to SetGameOver. The switch is refused in swap modes 3 and 5, in swap mode 0 with Tor selected and in swap mode 1 with Roberta selected, so in those modes a death on ordinary ground leads to game over even while the partner is alive (High).
- Module 1432 calls SetGameOver(1) once, inside its scripted ending (below). The call is not unconditional: the ending runs only when its gates pass. The argument value 1 is not a death.

- Hand-over. The game-over page appears on the pass after flag E is set. ### 5. Death callbacks

Registration. RegisterHeroDeathCallback(area object, callback, flag) stores the callback and the flag on the area object. Calling it again does not restart a callback that has already started. (Medium-High.)

Start guard. The gate starts the callback as a background task when the task handle on the area object is empty. The area's per-pass update frees a finished task and empties the handle when its live-area reaper predicate holds. A callback task does not run concurrently with an earlier callback task for the same area. The reaper runs when the area's own world is the world that is currently made current (the area stores its world at setup and the per-pass update compares it with the current world; High). So only the active area satisfies the reaper predicate: an inactive buffered area's callback is not advanced or reaped until that area becomes active again. A later eligible death can start a new callback after the earlier task finishes and the active-area update reaps it. Area reset clears the handle without using the normal per-handle task-release path. Buffer replacement reclaims its task storage and runs the module's bound Exit and unload hooks, but no task-specific cancellation/finalizer exists; what an unfinished task leaves behind is settled under Unfinished callback tasks below. A death while the task still runs starts nothing: the gate still overwrites the death-info record and returns the flag (flag 1: the hero is not killed; flag 0: the default death runs with no new callback). (High for active-only stepping, handle guard, completion cleanup and reset behavior; Medium-High for transition implications; see Open question 1.)

First slice (Medium-High). Starting the task only queues it; its first slice runs the next time the area's per-pass update steps the area's tasks, which is the first thing the script step of the current world does (SPEC-0026 main loop, the script step at the end of the world branch). So:
- A death from the engine side of the pass (an enemy or boss hit in the region pass, a projectile hit, a poison tick or a deadly-ground or overlay kill in the selected hero's update) runs the first slice in the same pass, later in that pass.
- A death from a level script (DamageHero or a kill inside a trigger callback, a zone helper or a control thread) happens after that step has passed, so the first slice runs in the script step of the next pass.
- Test vectors: an enemy blow takes the hero to 0 HP on pass N: the callback's first slice runs on pass N. A trigger callback's DamageHero(2) takes the hero from 2 HP to 0 on pass N: the first slice runs on pass N + 1.

Flag. The flag is read on every gate call. Flag 1 skips the default death when the callback gate returns it; flag 0 lets the default death run. A callback task does not overlap an earlier task for the same area. Later deaths can start a new callback only after the previous task ends and the live-area update reaps its handle (see Start guard and Open question 1).

Unfinished callback tasks (Medium-High). A task can be unfinished only if it waits, and only two callbacks wait: module 1176's (a 41-tick wait before it sets the death marker) and module 1394's (the checkpoint branch is a 48-tick fade-out, the move, a 60-tick wait and a 60-tick fade-in, about 168 ticks; the final-lever branch is 60 + 60 + 60 = 180 ticks). The callbacks of modules 735, 1098 and 1371 end in their first slice, so there is nothing left to clean up. No scripted load or door can start inside either window: 1176's world load is started by the module's death watcher after the marker is set, when the task has already ended, and 1394's only exit needs the room word that the shrine-room trigger sets, which the hero cannot reach within the window from a checkpoint (Medium). A second death cannot tear the area down either: in module 1394 a new death can come only after the move at tick 48 or later, and the game-over page then follows about 154 passes after it, when the window has closed; in module 1176 the hero is held in state 55 and never reaches the game-over choice. The only way left to discard an unfinished task is the player's own quit to the title or load of a save. Quit tears down both world buffers and then resets the world: the game-state record (timers, flags, world-enable), the screen effect (fade and letterbox bars), both heroes (start values, statuses cleared), the party inventory, the effect pools and the sound system; a load of a save resets the screen effect and reloads the world (SPEC-0026). What a discarded task had already done is covered by those resets: module 1394's task stops the on-screen countdown before its first wait (the timer is hidden and is never drawn again until a script activates it), its fade is cleared by the screen-effect reset, its move, camera return and fade-in simply do not happen, and the swap mode and control state are set again by the next world's own start. ### 7. Revive by script

NeedHeroAlive(h): if hero h is in the death state, the revive helper runs. The call returns 1 when a revive happens and 0 otherwise.

Revive helper:

1. Let P be the partner of the active hero.
2. If P is in the death state and the swap mode word equals 2, P is set to state 1 (idle) and P's health is raised by (maximum - current), so that health becomes the maximum. The helper returns 1.
3. Otherwise the helper returns 0 and changes nothing.

The revive acts only on a hero in the death state (High). A hero at zero health in state 17 is not revived by this call, and the call returns 0 for it.

Target (High). The helper always acts on the hero that is not selected; h only gates the call. So NeedHeroAlive(h) with h the selected hero revives nobody when the partner is alive, and revives the partner (not h) when both are dead. NeedHeroAlive with h the parked hero revives that hero. Test vectors (swap mode 2):
- Tor selected and in state 16, Roberta alive: NeedHeroAlive(0) changes nothing; Tor stays in state 16.
- Tor selected, Roberta parked in state 16 at 0 HP (max 120): NeedHeroAlive(1) sets Roberta to state 1 with 120 HP.
- Both in state 16, Tor selected: NeedHeroAlive(0) revives Roberta, not Tor.
- Swap mode 3: no call revives anyone.

### 8A. Deadly ground, the death-state handler and timers E and F

Confidence: High for the branches of the kill rule and its per-tick repeat, the death-state handler, the timer starters, the timer pass arithmetic, timer F, the state-60 penalty, the load arrival rule and the disc counts; Medium for the meaning of the surface values (Open questions 30).

Deadly-ground kill (each tick, in the motion update of the selected hero):

1. The rule applies to the player-controlled hero when the ground entry under it is deadly ground (kind 2, Layout) and its ground gap is 0.085 or less. The ground gap is the hero's position y minus the ground height below it (High).
2. Respawn index other than 0xFF: the hero's death hook runs with gate-allowed 1 (section 2), so a registered death callback is consulted as for a death from damage.
3. Respawn index 0xFF: both heroes are killed by the motion update's own kill-both rule, not by KillBothHeroes: the partner's death hook runs first, then the hook of the hero on the ground, both with gate-allowed 1 (High). A registered death callback is therefore consulted for each hero that is not already in the death state. The script call KillBothHeroes (section 6) is different and passes gate-allowed 0 to both heroes.
4. Health is not changed in either case.
5. The rule runs again on every tick the condition holds. From the second tick the hero is already in state 16, so each tick runs the repeated default death (section 2): state 16 is written again, the hit list and the status timers are cleared, and the death-cue test runs, so sound event 3 is requested again on each such tick while the partner is alive (High for the requests). The shared stinger handle suppresses new starts while the selected clip is active; repeated requests can start playback again only after the clip finishes and a later request reaches an available sound path (Open questions 34). The death voice is not replayed. The deep overlay rule of section 9 uses the same kill-both and repeats the same way.
6. Deadly ground deals no per-tick damage and no knockback. The other two ground rules of the motion tick (a hurting surface and a deep overlay kill) are in section 9.

Death-state handler (each tick while a hero is in the death state 16):

0. At the start of each handler tick the death mode is set to 0; the branches below set it again (1 or 2) in the same tick, so it is valid whenever the status machine or a placement reads it.
1. If the ground entry under the hero is deadly ground, the death animation and effect follow the surface word: surface 2 gives animation 173 with the one-shot effect 0x81; surface 8 gives animation 174 with the one-shot effect 0xBC; any other surface gives animation 136 with no effect. The effect starts on the tick the clip is requested, at the hero's position (effect group 3). Effect 129 (0x81) is a hot burst in three parts (an alpha blob thrown up, an additive glow and additive sparks, each a single burst). Effect 188 (0xBC) is a water splash: a burst of 35 child models, then 3 more every 0.2 s for 3 s. SPEC-0034 section 37 gives their emitter values (High for the values, Medium for the picture). Let i be the entry's respawn index.
   - i other than 0xFF: death mode = 2. If flag E is clear and timers E and F are both at or below 0 (off), timer F starts with index i.
   - i = 0xFF: death mode = 1, then step 3.
2. Any other ground (or none): the death animation is 34. When its progress reaches 0.95: if the partner is also in state 16, death mode = 1; otherwise a hero-switch request to the partner is made (SPEC-0053, no force). The request is refused in swap modes 3 and 5, in swap mode 0 with Tor selected and in swap mode 1 with Roberta selected; a refused request gives death mode = 1, so game over follows even with the partner alive. An accepted switch starts no timer. The request repeats on every later tick until the swap task changes the selected hero; this is harmless because a busy task slot ignores new starts. The swap gives the partner action state 1 and leaves the dead hero in state 16, not updated.
3. Death mode 1: if flag E is clear and timer E is at or below 0 (off), timer E starts (section 4). With timer E started, the death camera starts: SPEC-0027 "Death camera (mode 8 at game over)" gives it in full (mode 8 on the dying hero, radius 15, yaw offset 0, height 1.5, so distance 15.0748 and elevation 5.71 degrees; fractions 0.01; pivot step 0). The camera-mode-8 test only keeps it from re-entering mode 8 when the camera is already in that mode (High).

A hero in state 16 can be knocked out of it (R-0162; High for the code, Medium for the aftermath). The Ixi Chieftain's run-past push and whirl push test only whether the hero is already in state 48; neither tests state 16, and the state setter refuses nothing (SPEC-0036, A knock in a hold state). So the push replaces a dead hero's velocity with 3.0 away and 4.0 up and writes state 48 on the tick it lands. Health stays 0. The state-48 routine ends in state 1 on landing, but on deadly ground the kill rule above acts on the first tick the ground gap is 0.085 or less and writes state 16 again (the death voice plays again on that entry from another state). Timer E is not touched by a knock: it is started once by the handler (death mode 1, step 3, which needs flag E clear and timer E off) and counts on, so game over comes 91 additions after the handler's first tick on the slab whether or not the hero is knocked in between. On ordinary ground a knocked dead hero lands in state 1 with 0 health (Medium-Low) and the next hit that reaches him runs the death hook again. The Abandoned Mines furnace floor after the Chieftain's defeat (R-0214; High for the rules, Medium for the tick counts). The kill rule above applies unchanged after the win. State 16 summary (High): state 16 is the hero's death state. Entering it from another state plays the death voice (sound event 21 for Tor, 45 for Roberta; section 2). Its clip is 34 on ordinary ground, or 173 (surface word 2, effect 0x81), 174 (surface word 8, effect 0xBC) or 136 (any other surface) on deadly ground. The handler sets the death end mode: 2 for a respawn at the entry record's point (deadly ground with an index), 1 for game over (deadly ground without an index, or ordinary ground when the death clip reaches 0.95 and no partner takes over). Timers (game-state pass, once per pass):

- Each running timer adds dt (1/60 s, single precision) per pass. A timer acts on the first pass where its time is more than 1.5 s (strict). The single-precision sum of 90 additions is 1.4999993 and of 91 additions 1.5166659, so it acts on its 91st addition (High for the arithmetic; Medium-High for dt being exactly the single-precision 1/60).
- A timer started in the world branch of pass S is first advanced in the status machine of pass S+1, so it acts in the status machine of pass S+91, before that pass's world update.
- Deadly-ground kill on pass N (motion update): the handler runs on pass N+1 and starts timer F; the respawn happens in the status machine of pass N+92 (High).
- Timer E sets flag E and plays sound event 5 (section 4). It counts on every pass, also while the game is paused (section 4; Medium-High).
- Timer F: if the current world has entries and the index is below its entry count, the selected hero's start pose becomes that entry record's position and facing. The selected hero then respawns at its start pose without the heal (SPEC-0040, Behavior 1C): with death mode 2 its state becomes 60, and the death mode returns to 0. Then the index is set to 0xFF and the timer to off (-1). With an index out of range the start pose keeps its last value and the respawn still runs; no disc data does this.

Death-end states (High):

- State 60 (respawn after deadly ground): on its first tick it requests animation 137 and deducts D through the plain deduct (no hit cue, no reaction), with D = 5 when health is above 5 and D = health - 1 otherwise, that is D = min(5, health - 1). - Load arrival rule: every apply of a saved slot or of the session snapshot sets flag G. While flag G is set the game-state code is 1. The status machine counts the passes on which no load is busy (any busy pass resets the count to 0); after 6 such passes it clears flag G, starts an 8-frame fade-in from white (colour 255, 255, 255) and sends the selected hero state request 0x3B, which is state 59, the arrival state (SPEC-0026, Flags, timers and six-pass rules). ## Edge cases and error handling

- Kill quirk (reproduce): KillCurrentHero and KillBothHeroes leave health unchanged. A hero can therefore be in the death state with health above zero.
- Flag 1 without a reset (reproduce): after the first death, flag 1 keeps every later death in that area object from reaching the default death. In module 1176 the callback loops the death clip in state 55 and then holds the hero in state 33 at zero health until the hero-death cutscene and the reload of world 90 (section 5). In module 735 the active hero dies in the normal way (a kill-both partner keeps its health and state); in the post-fight window (flag 263 set, the post-fight word clear) the dying hero is moved to locator 5 and stays at zero health outside state 16. Hits still reach a hero that is not in the death state, so each hit runs the gate again. Do not assume a dead hero is always in the death state.
- Zero health outside state 16 (modules 1176, 735, 1371 and 1394): in module 1394 the callback's move puts the dying hero in state 1 at the checkpoint with 0 HP (Open question 41); in module 1176 the callback ends in the hero-death cutscene and the reload of world 90 (section 5). In module 1371 the first fallen hero stays parked at zero health until the round ends with the reload of world 157. Otherwise the zero health is cleared by a player heal item, by an act start (stage 2 heals Roberta only and the other stages heal both heroes; Placement, item 1), or by a heal call in another script. The revive helper does not clear it, because the revive acts only on state 16.
- Repeat direct kill (High): a direct kill of a hero already in the death state runs the default death again, cue test included (Behavior 2, step 1). A hit does nothing to such a hero except play the hit cue and effect (Behavior 1, step 3).
- Start guard: a callback task does not overlap an earlier task for the same area. A later eligible death can start a new callback after the earlier task finishes and the live-area update reaps it, if the live-area reaper predicate holds. This gives one callback per eligible death only under that condition; the predicate's value during play needs a runtime check (Open question 1; rewrite choice: treat it as true while the area is current). A death while a task is still running does not start another task.
- Module 1371: after the callback switches control and sets swap mode 3, the first fallen hero is parked at 0 HP outside the death state (flag 1), and swap mode 3 keeps it parked. The second fall ends the round (section 5). Test vector: Tor falls first, then Roberta: Tor parked at 0 HP in state 17, Roberta in state 55, flag 595 set, then the reload of world 157.
- Item heal at zero health (High for the paths): the heal branch of the item routine has no state test. The quick-item panel's use refuses a hero in state 16 and allows a hero at 0 HP in any other state. The item screen's use and the script call UseItemFromPlayerInventory have no state test. So a heal item raises a 0 HP hero outside state 16 (it is forced, so it heals whenever health is below the maximum), and through the item screen it also raises the health of a selected hero in state 16, which stays in state 16. Test vector: a hero at 0 of 120 HP in state 17 uses a heal item worth 60 HP from the quick panel: 60 HP, state unchanged.
- Health gain in the death state (High): every "dead" test in the game (the switch and game-over tests of the death-state handler, the death cue, the revive helper, the callback gate's both-dead flag, the HUD dead icon) reads the action state (state 16), not the health, and the death-state handler has no health test. So a downed hero whose health rises (the petpet point of SPEC-0047, an item heal through the item screen or a script heal) stays down: it does not get up, it still counts as dead for the partner switch and for game over, and it can leave state 16 only through the exits listed in this spec (the revive, a respawn or world-load placement, the Continue reset, or an active stun). Test vector: Roberta in state 16 at 0 HP gains 1 HP per second from a petpet for 10 s: 10 HP, still in state 16; with Tor also in state 16, game over still follows.
- Equal damage is a kill: damage equal to health sets health to 0 and runs the death gate.
- Five percent rule: at a maximum of 200, the threshold is 10. Damage 10 is light (10 is not more than 10); damage 11 is heavy.
- SetGameOver ignores its argument.
- An entry index out of range makes SetHeroStartPos do nothing. An index in range stores the start pose of both heroes and moves no one.
- Timer F with an index at or above the current world's entry count (no disc data does this): the start pose is not changed, and the hero respawns at its previous start pose (reproduce).
- Deadly ground and health: the kill does not change health and the timer F respawn does not heal, so a hero comes back in state 60 with its health before the kill minus min(5, health - 1): 5 HP less, never below 1 (section 8A).
- Two deaths in the same tick: the death cue test sees each hero's state at the moment of its own call (Behavior 2, step 4d), so the cue depends on call order.
- Input lock: a hit that arrives while the input-lock word is set (a dialogue box or a list picker is up) is discarded. It is not queued.
- Standing on deadly ground after the kill (reproduce): the kill runs again on every tick, each time a repeated default death with a request of sound event 3 while the partner is alive; the death voice plays only once (section 8A).
- Poison on a hero at 0 HP outside state 16 (reproduce; Medium-High): a hero left at 0 HP by a flag-1 callback keeps its statuses. At the next poison reading the poison damage is health - 1 = -1, and the deduct subtracts -1, so health becomes 1.
- Stun on a dead hero (Medium): a status apply has no death gate, and an active stun forces state 1 in the hero tick, so a stun applied to a hero in state 16 would take it out of the death state at 0 HP. No known source targets a dead hero with a stun (Open questions 39).
- World load while dead: every world-load placement takes a dead selected hero out of state 16 (state 59 or 1) without healing it (section 8, item 6).
- Second death while a callback task is running (High for the code; Medium-High for play): the gate overwrites the death-info record and returns the flag but starts nothing. With flag 1 the hero is not killed. With flag 0 (modules 1098 and 1394) the default death runs without a callback. In module 1394 a hit that lands between the callback's move (about tick 48 of the task) and the task's end (about tick 168) puts the hero at the checkpoint back in state 16 with no callback to move it again; swap mode 3 then refuses the take-over at the 0.95 point and the game ends. A hit after the task is reaped starts the callback again.
- Death-info record under a kill-both rule (High): the gate writes the record on both calls (the partner first, then the hero on the ground), so a callback started by the first call reads the second call's values when its first slice runs: the selected hero's id, and a both-dead flag of 0 when the callback has skipped the default death.

## Open questions

Default if unresolved (rewrite choice) for a boss knock on a hero in state 16 (R-0162): apply the knock (velocity and state 48) exactly as to a living hero, leave timer E running, and let the deadly-ground kill rule write state 16 on the next tick the ground gap is 0.085 or less; no run-time check of the original is available.

Area reset clears the handle without releasing the task through the normal per-handle release path.
