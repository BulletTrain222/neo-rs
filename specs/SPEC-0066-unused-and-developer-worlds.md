# SPEC-0066: Unused, cut and developer worlds (the 17 world packs a new game never enters)

- Answers: proactive (unused worlds pass); proactive (verify pass)
- Status: Draft
- Disc build: SCUS-97367 v1.00. World packs are archive resources 532 to 1433; area slot tables A and B as in SPEC-0006. Check values for a reader: slot 0 holds pack 678 and world id 0; slot 5 holds pack 691 and world id 11; slot 68 holds pack 909 and world id 602.
- Confidence: High for every disc fact (sizes, bounds, counts, positions, byte equality, ground heights); High for the module behaviours quoted from SPEC-0032, SPEC-0037, SPEC-0041 and SPEC-0042; Medium for the classes "developer test world" (1259, 1298) and "alternate build" (684, 1003, 1323), which rest on layout, numbering and content, not on a label; Medium for the start-slot words (Developer hooks); Low for why any pack was left out of the route graph.

## Summary

The game ships 191 world packs, and a new game can enter 174 of them (SPEC-0006, Reach). This spec describes the other 17 so that a rewrite can load and show each one as an optional extra. It also records unrequested region resources associated with reachable areas: the previously catalogued groups at packs 722 and 1327, a Drackonack Fang pickup in resource 815, alternate Drack Ridge contents in resources 894 and 899, a Prince Tourin placement in resource 773, and eight more character resources that no level module loads (697, 698, 700, 701, 729, 1325, 1326 and 1349). None can be loaded in the shipped game (no level module names them and no other loader reads character regions); why they exist cannot be known from the data. It also records that pack 1170 holds four treasure chests, and that the six no-drop breakables of pack 874 are three body-and-remnant pairs. For every pack it gives the kind (which world buffer it goes into), the zone and map ids, the size and bounds, what ground, collision and render data it has, which textures and models are unique to it, what is placed in it, what its level module does, where a hero can be put, whether it stands alone, a class, and a recipe. It also lists the developer and test content that exists in the data (a debug start-point chooser, a debug flag that is always off, button-driven debug branches in modules, and a start-slot hook that nothing sets) and shows that there are no other world packs on the disc.

The three classes are: **A** worth adding (content found nowhere else); **B** duplicate or near-duplicate (the difference is stated exactly); **C** stub (empty, tiny or broken; what breaks is stated).

Two readings in earlier specs change. Pack 1259 is not a cut variant of the 909 surroundings: it is a developer showroom with one exhibit per character group (38 exhibits, 205 placements), loaded exhibit by exhibit when the hero steps on a trigger sphere. Pack 1298 is a developer climbing and movement course. Pack 1323 is a second, richer build of the tower stairwell of pack 1427, not an unused copy of it.

## Where it lives on the disc

- **Packs.** Each pack is one archive resource whose first chunk is type 31 (SPEC-0006). Its character regions are the resources 1 to K after it that hold a type-36 chunk; K is the first u32 at offset 4 of the pack's first tagged record, minus 1. Region j of pack P is resource P + 1 + j.
- **Table rows.** Slot, world id, zone id, map area and sound of each pack are in the table below. The slot is the row in table A; the world id is the same row of table B; zone id, map area and sound are in the pack's type-1001 chunk (SPEC-0006, Zone id).
- **Area-pair table** (data address 0x31EEE0, SPEC-0006): it lists the pairs (909, 1259), (909, 1298), (956, 970), (1023, 1077) and (1077, 1080) among others. These five pairs are the only table entries that name packs outside the reached set, and the packs are 1259, 1298, 970 and 1077.
- **Start words** (data addresses 0x2D7560 and 0x2D7564, SPEC-0006, Default start): both always hold -1.
- **Other tables.** The no-swap list (data address 0x31EEA0) and the block-pair list (0x31EE80) name none of the 17 packs.
- **All world packs.** Exactly 192 resources begin with a type-31 chunk. 191 are in table A and the 192nd is the front-end pack 532. No other resource that starts with a chunk header holds a type-19, type-11 or type-1001 chunk, so there is no hidden world outside the 191 game packs and the front end.

## Behavior

### Common loading recipe (High)

1. Choose the buffer from the kind: transition packs go into T, exteriors into E, interiors into I (SPEC-0006, World kinds). An interior can be entered only while E is resident and current.
2. Run the normal pack load (chunk walk, grid, model sets, type-11 spawn vector, level module Init). Nothing in these 17 packs needs a special case for loading.
3. The hero is placed by the post-load rule of SPEC-0006 (Hero placement): request plane -1 to land on entry record 0, or set a start pose from any entry record. The ground height under an entry is the ground probe at the entry's horizontal position with ceiling y + 8 (y + 6 for an interior entry); when the probe finds ground the hero's y becomes that height, otherwise the entry's y stays.
4. Make the pack current. A pack whose cells name neighbours streams them in by the pending step as in play; a rewrite can suppress that for a stand-alone view.
5. Leave by an exit listed in the world's section, or by a return that the rewrite adds. The safe baseline return is the new-game start: reload 909 into E and land on its entry record 0.

The table of start poses with ground heights is in Test vectors.

### Duplicates: 678, 686, 970, 1077 (class B, High)

- **678 and 686** equal 691 chunk for chunk (grid, model sets, collision, module, embedded region, save point). Their regions (679 to 682 and 687 to 690) equal 692 to 695 in size and content. The only differences are the table row (world id 0 and 9 against 11) and the unused bytes after the last chunk. The zone lookup of both returns 691. The module is the road-fight module of 691 (SPEC-0042, 678). Pack 684's neighbour list names world 0, which is 678; the list of pack 1184 also holds world 0, but no cell of 1184 selects that position. Nothing else names 678 or 686.
- **970** is byte-identical to 956 as a whole resource, and its 13 regions (971 to 983) equal 957 to 969. Its table row has world id 905 against 902. The area-pair table pairs 956 with 970, so the pending step never streams one from the other.
- **1077** equals 1129 chunk for chunk (regions: none). The only differences are the table row (world id 1325 against 1370) and the bytes after the last chunk. Its module is written for 1129's slot (109): it reads the blackboard values keyed by slot 109 and loads slot 90. Loaded as 1077 it would read the wrong keys, so a rewrite that wants the Ancient Sewers scene should load 1129. 1077 is paired with 1023 and with 1080 in the area-pair table.
- **Recipe.** Do not add them as separate worlds. If the rewrite lists them, map each to its twin (691, 956, 1129) and keep the twin's world id.

### Pack 1259: character showroom (class A, High for the data, Medium for the intent)

**What it is.** An exterior with a flat test floor and 38 exhibits laid out on a 50-unit lattice. It has no map area (-1), zone and world id 3000, sound row 0, and is paired with 909 in the area-pair table. The grid has 713 cells that carry a value (650 of them with entries), and all hold neighbour value 1, which names world 602 (pack 909); the pair rule stops that stream, so it is harmless.

**Ground, collision, render.** One ground patch (x -161 to 161, z -241 to 161; height 0 with a rise to about 1.3 at z 150); the grid reaches z 714 but cells outside the patch return the empty-cell height 0.0, so the whole grid is walkable at height 0. No deadly ground. Collision is the 38 trigger spheres and one door; no walls. Display tree 456 KB, an object-mesh container of 608 KB, one 204 KB texture (all unique to this pack), and a 4.8 KB navigation mesh.

**Placements.** 38 character regions, resources 1260 to 1297 (region j = trigger j). 205 placements, 165 distinct monster rows, 92 monster types; 79 are hostile enemies, the rest are friendly characters and scenery types. Regions 1263, 1267 and 1269 also hold 4, 5 and 4 summon-reserved records (position zero, bound by spawn-by-key). Exhibits 0 to 17 are the enemy and boss groups (j = 0 Undead Draik; 2 Flower Monsters; 4 Werelupe King, Gnarfas and Werelupes; 5 spiders; 6 Drackonacks and a Flying Minion; 7 plant monsters; 9 the six Minion kinds; 10 Death Knight and skeletons; 11 Slorgs and Sludgy; 12 Crokabeks; 14 Tentacles; 15 Ixi; 16 road brigands; 17 Dark Knights, spellbooks and Spirit Swords); exhibits 18 to 37 are mostly the townsfolk and story-character types, one type per exhibit with its variants (exhibits 20 and 21 hold the special types 100 to 109). 28 placements use 12 monster rows that no other pack places: rows 9, 11, 69, 71, 72, 73, 81, 99, 111, 121, 131 and 176. Items: 20 item points (item ids 98 to 117, flag 0, one per id) on a 6.7-unit lattice at x 94 to 120, z -194 to -162. No chests, ladders or ledges.

**Entry records** (5): (-50, 0, -50), (0, 0, 100), (0, 0, 150), (50, 0, 150), (50, 0, 100), all facing +z. **Teleport plane** (1): door 0 at (-7.84, -7.58, -193.2), normal +z, 15.8 wide, 19.75 high, arrival door 1.

**Module (SPEC-0037, 1259).** Init creates one slot with 50 candidate sets and registers script trigger indices 0 to 49 with one callback. **Stands alone?** Yes. It needs no neighbour.

**How to add it.**

- Story state: none. Flags: none.
- Exits: door 0 is a teleport plane with the default rule: the current pack is paired with 909, so E is replaced by 909 and the hero lands on 909's teleport plane 1. This works only if 909 is loadable; the rewrite's own return does the same job.
- Hazards: none deadly. The exhibits need the monster-row and character-model loaders of the main game; exhibits 18 to 37 use townsfolk models whose names are empty (the type decides the look).
- Spawn and ground: (-50, 0, -50) with ground 0.0.

## Edge cases and error handling

- **No walkable ground (683).** The ground probe returns the empty-cell value 0.0 where a cell has no entries, so a hero placed above 683 falls to height 0.0 and keeps walking on that plane; over the two pools the deadly ground applies and, with no respawn entry (index 0xFF), ends the game (SPEC-0055).
- **Falling out of a world.** In 1003, 684, 742, 879 and 1323 the grid is the size of the scenery and the terrain is higher than 0, so leaving the patches drops the hero to 0.0 with no death and no way up. The original keeps heroes in with wall volumes; a rewrite may add a bounds check that returns the hero to the world's entry 0.
- **Door that cannot land.** 1298's door asks for door 2 of 909, which 909 does not have: the landing is refused and the hero lands on entry record 0 of 909. 1003's door and cells name 1017, an exterior that would be entered as an interior.
- **Hidden objects (1298).** Objects 1 to 6 are never drawn, and their patches never count in the ground probe.
- **Empty and shared modules.** The module of 1003 (and 1004, 1082) does nothing; loading 1077 runs a module that expects slot 109.
- **Transition with a transition neighbour (684).** The pending step loads nothing; the hero stays in 684.
- **Streaming surprise (742).** Standing on any non-zero cell loads 735 into E.
- **Interior entry (1170).** The enter call is refused unless E is current; the leave call does nothing unless I is current.
- **Table twin.** Looking a zone id up returns the first matching row: 1077's zone returns 1129's row, 970's returns 956's.
