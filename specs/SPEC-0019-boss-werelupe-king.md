## Summary

The Werelupe King is a large melee boss. Each game tick it chooses a movement (walk toward the hero, orbit around the hero, or hop back), and it may start one of several attacks: a block, a jump, a roll, a boomerang throw, a volley of flame waves, a swing, a lunge, or a three-hit combo. It also flinches when the hero hits it and triumphs while the hero is down. Block, jump and roll are answers to the hero's own attack (a rising reaction trigger), and the jump is also tested on every tick as a return-home move when the hero comes within 5 units of a boss that has strayed more than 15 units from home; the others are picked on the boss's own timers. Its health sets three phases. Each attack has a cooldown, a distance window and a chance that depends on the phase. Phase 2 and phase 3 set the controller's speed word to 1.2 and 1.4, and the controller copies that word into the per-tick speed scale P at the top of every pass, so while the controller runs every clip, and the root travel that follows it, plays 20 percent faster in phase 2 and 40 percent faster in phase 3 (G15; this replaces the earlier reading that the words reach nothing). The scenes the arena script plays on the King are not sped up, because the controller does not run during them. The boss moves only by the root motion of the clips it plays, the same way the heroes and every other character do (SPEC-0056, Travel by root motion). All timings run on the 60 Hz game tick (SPEC-0007): a time given in seconds becomes ticks = trunc(seconds x 60). The flame-wave wait is given in frames directly.

## Fight AI build guide

This is the ordered recipe for the whole shipped fight (pack 1246). Each item points to the detailed section that holds the rule, its confidence and its edge cases. Confidence is High unless marked. A tick is 1/60 s (SPEC-0007). "Run n" is the n-th pass of the King's controller; run 1 is the tick on which the King is bound (Activation in play). "T" is the tick on which a state is entered; its handler runs on that same tick (G3, step 6), so the first animation request of a state is made on tick T. "Gate m" means that an animation requested on tick T passes its 0.99 progress gate so that the handler sees it finished on tick T + m and takes its exit on that tick; the state therefore occupies ticks T to T + m, and the next state's first tick is T + m + 1.

### G1. Scope of the shipped fight

- Every controller state (0 to 20) occurs except big laugh (state 3), whose request word is never set. The big-laugh chance and the three exhausted-pant percents are never read. Action ids 14, 15 and 28 of the King's action table are never requested by the controller or by a script.
- Phase 2 and phase 3 are real: attack chances change, a scripted scene plays, and the King's play rate rises to R = 1.2 (phase 2) and R = 1.4 (phase 3) while its controller runs (G15). Every tick count in this guide that depends on a clip is stated at R = 1 (phase 1); G15 gives the rule and a table for the other two phases.
- The King of pack 985 is held by its arena script for the whole Gnarfas fight and never acts (Activation in play). It is not part of this guide.

### G3. Per-tick order (one controller pass per tick; the pass does not run while the King's base state is 33, 16, 25, 36, 42, 53 or 55)

1. Top of the pass: store the King's id in the selected hero's forced lock-on target field (+0x15E0; the hero's lock choice uses a forced target once and resets it, SPEC-0040 Behavior 20, so while the King's controller runs the hero's lock-on goes to the King; Medium-High), clear the King's unhittable word (+0x7F0) to 0, set base state 1, and set the time scale P to the controller's speed word (1.0 until the phase 2 entry, 1.2 after it, 1.4 after the phase 3 entry; G15).
2. Pre-check, in this order, a later step replacing the state chosen by an earlier one: hit flinch (reaction word 10: clear it, state 1, pick action 0x0B, 0x0C or 0x0D with probability 1/3 each); catch request (state 2, action 0x14); big laugh (never); triumph (hero down, state free, not already state 4: state 4, action 0x12); health: fraction 0 or less gives state 20 on every tick, else below 0.37 enters the phase 3 entry (state 6), else below 0.75 enters the phase 2 entry (state 5) (Phases and health thresholds; the entries also hold the King, G10). Gates: the flinch, the catch, the phase entries and death have no state gate and cut whatever the King is doing (an attack in progress is dropped on the spot); only the triumph needs a free state (so an attack in progress finishes first) and "hero down" means the selected hero is in its death state. The flinch word is cleared as soon as it is read, so a catch, triumph or phase entry that replaces the flinch on the same tick leaves that hit without a flinch animation.
3. Reaction, only on the tick on which the hero's attack predicate turns true: if the state is free, block; then if still free, jump; then if still free, roll (G6).
4. Attack selectors in this order: boomerang, flame wave, swing, lunge, combo. Each updates its own cooldown counter first, whatever the state, and then needs a free state.
5. Movement selector: it first runs the jump chooser again (every tick, not only on the hero's attack edge), then walk, hop back, orbit (G7).
6. Run the handler of the current state (G5). A handler of a state that was chosen earlier in the same tick runs on that tick.
7. Boomerang flight step, flame ring step, debug text (no effect), then wait one tick.

"Free" states are 0 idle, 1 flinch, 16 walk, 17 hop back, 18 and 19 orbit. All other states (2 to 15 and 20) are closed to every selector (Attack selection). Because the flinch is a free state, any attack whose gates pass on the tick after a hit replaces the flinch at once.

### G6. Reaction and attack choice (priority order)

1. Hero's attack edge (the hero attack-state test of SPEC-0040, Behavior 18: Tor in action state 13 with sub-state 2 to 12, or Tor's plunge in the jump states; Roberta only in action state 13 with sub-state 5 or 12, so her ordinary shots, spins and leap start nothing). The edge fires once when the test turns true. If the King is busy at that moment, the edge is used up and the swing is missed.
   - Block (ready flag 1, free): roll 0 to 100 below 25 / 35 / 75 for phases 1 / 2 / 3, at any hero distance. Block start: state 7, request 0x19.
   - Jump (ready flag 1, free): hero closer than 5, King farther than 15 from home, roll below c = 5 + (home distance - 15) / 13 x 75 (G7).
   - Roll (free; no ready flag, no distance test): roll below 50; a coin picks action 0x09 or 0x0A.
   - Taking the three in turn, a phase 1 swing with the King free starts a block with probability 25/101, a roll with 76/101 x 50/101 (about 0.373), nothing with about 0.380 (the jump needs the home distance).
2. Boomerang (ready flag, free, hero distance above 15 and below 40): cooldown 8 s, roll below 90 / 50 / 25.
3. Flame wave (ready flag, free, hero distance above 10 and below 40): cooldown 10 s, roll below 0 / 50 / 75 (no waves in phase 1).
4. Swing (ready flag, free, hero closer than 10): cooldown 10 s, roll below 60 / 46 / 20.
5. Lunge (ready flag, free, hero closer than 10): cooldown 8 s, roll below 30 / 40 / 50.
6. Combo (ready flag, free, hero closer than 10): cooldown 10 s, roll below 10 / 20 / 30.
7. Movement (G7).

Cooldown rule, exactly: each selector begins with its counter. If the counter is at or below cooldown x 60 (480 for 8 s, 600 for 10 s) it adds one and the selector ends; otherwise it goes on to the state, ready-flag, distance and roll tests. The counter therefore stops at 481 or 601, rises in every state (selectors run in all states except while the controller is held) and is zeroed when its attack starts (swing, lunge, combo), when the catch ends (boomerang) and when the volley ends (flame wave). The swing counter is zeroed a second time on the tick the swing state ends (this also applies to the roll counter-attack, which runs in the swing state), so the swing gap is counted from the swing's exit tick: a swing of action 0x17 started on S ends on S + 73 and its next roll is on S + 73 + 602 = S + 675; for action 0x16 the exit tick is S + 69 and the next roll S + 671. The lunge and combo counters are zeroed only at their start (the lunge gap counts from E, the combo gap from E, not from their ends). The first roll is on run 482 for the boomerang and lunge and on run 602 for the swing, combo and flame wave; after a restart at tick E the next roll is on tick E + 482 or E + 602. A roll is an integer 0 to 100, so "below c" succeeds with probability c / 101 on each tick on which the gates pass; this is a per-tick chance, not a per-choice chance. Because the five selectors run in this order on the same tick, a boomerang or flame wave start closes the later selectors for that tick.

Block (state 7) in detail: every tick the King turns 0.05 toward the hero and sets base state 20. On the first tick it requests 0x19. After that, in this order each tick: (1) if the one-shot "blocked" word (+0x8BC, set when a swing of the hero is blocked) is set, clear it, request recovery 0x1D (ticks T' to T' + 29, still guarding) and then idle; (2) else, while the hero's attack predicate is true, keep holding; (3) else (the hero's attack has ended): if the roll (0 to 100) is below 25, the hero is closer than 5 and the hero's attack sub-state word is 13, start the overhead counter (state 11, action 0x18, once flag cleared); otherwise idle. The sub-state 13 requirement means the overhead counter follows only a hero who is in the recoil sub-state (Tor after a blocked swing, or Roberta's leap landing); after a blocked Tor swing the blocked word takes the recovery branch first, so in practice the overhead counter is rare (Medium).

Draws and the rarely seen attacks (High). (1) Every selector spends its draw (an integer 0 to 100) on each tick on which all its earlier gates pass, whatever the chance: in phase 1 the flame-wave selector still draws against chance 0, so a port that skips the draw advances the generator differently from the original. (2) On the hero's attack edge the order is block, then jump, then roll, each re-testing the free state, so a failed block draw is followed by the jump test (draws only if the hero is within 5 and the King is more than 15 from home) and then by the roll draw; with the ready flag 0 (boomerang out) the block and jump spend no draw. (3) The roll coin: 0 picks action 0x09, 1 picks action 0x0A. (4) A hero standing still never starts a block, roll or jump, because they need the hero's attack edge (or, for the jump, a hero within 5 of a King more than 15 from home). The King walks only while the hero is farther than 25 and stops under 25, and it orbits only under 10, so a standing hero between 10 and 25 away (the intro leaves it at 10.65) meets no swing, lunge or combo (they need under 10), no flame wave in phase 1 (chance 0) and no boomerang (it needs 15 to 40) below 15. A hero held inside 10 from the first running tick meets the lunge from the 482nd pass (counter above 480) and the swing and combo from the 602nd (counter above 600); the orbit lets the distance grow, its square rising about 0.0625 per tick, so from 6.0 the hero stays inside 10 for about 1000 ticks and from 8.0 for about 570. Test vectors (phase 1, King idle, ready flag 1, hero standing): swing counter 601, lunge and combo counters 0, hero 6.0 away: draw 37 starts the swing on that tick (action 0x17); all three counters at their caps and draws 77, 55, 4: swing and lunge fail, the combo starts (state 15); lunge counter 481, others 0, draw 12: lunge starts (state 14); hero attack edge with draws 63 (block fails), 31 (roll passes) and coin 1: roll with action 0x0A (the jump spends no draw with the hero beyond 5); King 21.0 from home, hero 4.0 from the King, draw 20 (chance 39.6): jump starts (state 8, action 6). Phase 2 at 599 of 800, flame counter 601, boomerang counter 0, hero 12.0 away, draw 33 (chance 50): flame wave starts (state 13, action 0x1A), its first ring at 40 ticks.

### G7. Movement

- Jump chooser, every tick (also on the hero's attack edge): needs ready flag 1, free state, hero closer than 5 (horizontal), King farther than 15 from home (horizontal); the start roll 0 to 100 must be below 5 + (d - 15) / 13 x 75 with d the home distance (39.6 at d = 21; every roll at d of 31.47 or more). This makes the jump the King's leash: a hero who comes within 5 units of a King that has wandered more than 15 from home sets off a jump home. Jump timing: G5 and Jump flight.
- Walk: hero farther than 25 (strict, horizontal) from any free state except the flinch: state 16, locomotion mode 0, action 2, moving along the walk mesh toward the hero's position with the navigation step of SPEC-0056 (Navigation step, Steering and turn rate), travelling 0.10625 units per tick (6.37 per second) by root motion. Walk ends on the first handler tick with distance below 25 (idle).
- Hop timer: the movement selector reaches the timer only when the state is free and not the flinch and the hero is within 25 (strict: not above 25). On each such tick it adds one first, then, if the hero is closer than 5 and the ready flag is 0 (a boomerang is out) and the new value is above 60, the timer is cleared (hit or miss) and a roll below 50 enters hop back (state 17, action 0x05). The timer is cleared nowhere else and never counts down, so it keeps its value through attacks and holds. A failed hop roll falls through to the orbit test of the same tick. The movement selector of the next tick finds the hero closer than 10 and the state not an orbit and picks an orbit, so a hop back lasts one tick and its root motion is negligible (the first key of the clip has almost no travel) (Medium-High).
- Orbit: hero closer than 10 (strict), state not already 18 or 19: a coin picks the right or left orbit (state 18 or 19, locomotion mode 5 or 6). The King faces the hero throughout (strafe steering: blend 0.1, or a snap when the facing is already within a dot product of 0.9 of the hero direction, SPEC-0040 Behavior 8a) and travels 0.25012 units per tick (15.01 per second) along its own side axis, so the distance to a hero who stands still grows slowly: its square grows by about 0.0625 per tick (Medium). An orbit has no timer. It ends only by walk (hero beyond 25), hop back, a new attack, a flinch, a block/roll/jump on the hero's attack edge, or when the target point 5 units to the side is not on the walk mesh (then a jump home starts). The mesh test runs every orbit tick: the point (position plus 5 times the side vector, or minus it) is searched in every triangle of the King's walk mesh (horizontal inside test, height ignored). If no triangle holds it, the jump starts at once with no ready-flag, free-state or chance test and no distance-to-home condition, so an orbiting King with less than about 5 units of floor on its orbit side jumps toward home. The orbit coin is a draw of 0 or 1: 0 gives the left orbit (state 19, mode 6, action 4) and 1 the right orbit (state 18, mode 5, action 3). At hero distances from 10 to 25 an orbit that is running continues; an idle King at those distances stays idle.
- Between 10 and 25 with no orbit running the King stands in idle, turning toward the hero at blend 0.05 per tick; its attacks other than the boomerang and the flame waves are out of range.
- Turn weights: idle, block and flame wave 0.05 per tick; jump launch and air 0.2; combo hits snap to the hero (weight 1.0) on each animation's gate tick only. The swing, lunge, overhead counter and boomerang throw do not steer: their direction is the facing at their start. A damaging hit from the hero snaps the King to face the hero (weight 1.0).
- Unreachable hero: the King has no special rule; the walk follows the mesh route table toward the hero and stops at a boundary edge (SPEC-0056, Route step). The jump chooser and the orbit-blocked rule are its only returns home.

### G9. Flame wave and boomerang timing

- Wave k starts (clip requested) on tick S_k with S_0 = the state's entry tick; the ring is started on tick S_k + 48 (key 12, the single key with bit 0x400) if the fired flag is clear; the clip's gate is S_k + 89. On the gate tick the wave index goes up (or the volley ends), the sub-animation becomes 0, and a wait W is drawn uniformly from 0 to 15. Tick +1: request 0x00; from tick +2 the wait counts down once per tick and, when it is below 1, the sub-animation becomes 0x1A; the tick after that requests it. So S_(k+1) = S_k + 92 + W (W = 0 to 15). The last wave (index 3 in phases 1 and 2, index 4 in phase 3) ends on its gate tick S_last + 89, when the counter is zeroed and the state is idle. A volley lasts 366 to 411 ticks in phase 2 (4 waves, three W draws) and 458 to 518 ticks in phase 3 (5 waves, four W draws); the King does not leave state 13 for its whole length (a hit flinch can).
- Each ring lives about 48 ticks from S_k + 48 (Flame ring), is centred where the King stood, and deals 15 once per ring when it passes the hero's distance band and height.

### G11. Things a port gets wrong easily

- Using clip 0 or a fixed duration for an action: every requested id has its own clip; durations are 4 ticks per key (G4).
- Cooldowns: the counter stops at cooldown x 60 + 1 and the first roll is on run 482 or 602, counted from the zeroing tick. Chances are per tick and out of 101 values.
- The jump chooser runs every tick (G7); the hop back is replaced by the orbit on the next tick; the orbit never ends by itself; a still hero at 10 to 25 units is never approached or attacked at close range.
- Distances: horizontal for every choice; the reach test is three-dimensional (65.0 gate).
- Hurt side: the King cannot be hit during flinch, catch, roll and jump, nor while held; while it blocks, a swing from the hero's front is blocked and one from behind has no effect; the first damaging hit is doubled (Hurt side).
- Phase entries hold the controller until the arena script releases it. After the release the King plays 1.2 times (phase 2) or 1.4 times (phase 3) as fast as in phase 1: shorter gates, faster walk and orbit, shorter flame volleys (G15). A port that keeps R = 1.0 makes the second half of the fight noticeably easier than the original.
- A blocked King hit on the hero still uses the once flag; boss hits are raw (not reduced by armour or shield).
- The boomerang does not stop the King: ready flag 0 closes the boomerang, flame, swing, lunge and combo selectors and the block and jump choosers, but not the roll and not the movement.
- The swing cooldown runs from the swing's end, the other attack cooldowns from their start (G6).
- The catch, the flinch, the phase entries and death have no free-state gate; the triumph does (G3).
- Boomerang and flame ring damage never goes through the hero's guard; only the melee attacks (swing, lunge, overhead counter, combo) can be blocked (G8).
- The King's hurt volumes are two bone capsules (the body and the head), not a cylinder, for the hero's swing and Roberta's shots; only the Mote objects use the body cylinder of radius 3.0 and height 6.0 (Hurt side).
- More edge cases and the hero-side effects are in G13 and G14.

### G13. Edge cases for implementers (easy to miss)

1. One target. Every King test reads the selected hero at the moment of the call (G2). A swap mid-attack changes the target for the rest of that attack (the combo's snap turns, the boomerang's re-aimed hero point, the flame wave's turn) from the next tick.
2. Ungated pre-check steps. Flinch, catch, phase entries and death replace any state at once (G3). A catch can cut a roll, an orbit, a walk or a triumph; a phase entry can cut an attack in progress (its once flag and the cooldown counters stay as they are).
3. Cooldown counters. All five rise by one on every pass until they pass their limit (481 or 601) and freeze while the controller is held. The swing counter is zeroed at the swing's start and again at its end; the other four are zeroed as listed in G6.
4. First rolls. After the bind the first boomerang or lunge roll is on run 482 and the first swing, combo or flame roll on run 602. After the intro hold the counters have kept their values from before the hold (a hold freezes them), so the fight does not restart them.
5. Hop timer (G7). The timer is incremented before it is compared; it only counts on movement ticks that reach it. It is cleared by the hop roll, hit or miss. A failed hop roll continues to the orbit test on the same tick.
6. Orbit near an edge. The orbit target is re-aimed every tick, 5 units to the side; any tick on which that point has no floor under it (walk mesh) starts an ungated jump toward home. A King fighting within about 5 units of the arena edge on the orbit side therefore keeps jumping home.
7. Jump chooser. It runs every tick and in the flinch (a free state): a hero within 5 of a King that is more than 15 from home can interrupt a flinch with a jump. The orbit-blocked jump skips every gate.
8. Reaction edge. The block, jump and roll rolls are made once per hero attack, on the tick the hero's attack-state test turns true (the start of a Tor swing step or a Roberta swat, before the weapon window opens). If the King is busy on that tick, the edge is lost. The test stays true while the hero stays in an attack sub-state, so a chain of swings is one edge unless the hero leaves the attack states between them.
9. Block lifetime. The King guards (base state 20) on every tick of state 7, including the 0x1D recovery. It holds while the hero's attack test is true, drops to idle when it ends (or goes to the overhead counter, rarely), and goes to recovery on the tick after a hero swing was blocked. A hit that goes through to the King's take-hit while it blocks (a Mote object, a full-release shot, a power step, a poison or fire tick) does not cancel the block on that tick but sets the flinch word, and the next pre-check replaces the block by a flinch.
10. First-hit doubling. The first call of the King's take-hit doubles the damage, whatever its source (a poison or fire tick uses it up like a hero hit). A blocked swing never calls take-hit, so it does not use it up. While a stun status runs on the King the doubling applies to every hit (G14).
11. Hit timing. A hit on tick t starts the flinch on t + 1 and the King is next hittable at t + 39 (action 0x0B), t + 47 (0x0C) or t + 43 (0x0D), unless a free-state selector replaces the flinch earlier; the unhittable word is set on every tick of the flinch, the catch, the roll and the jump, including the exit tick.
12. Melee hit windows. The King's hit needs the animation event bit on the current frame, the clip below 0.99 progress, a 3D distance of at most 65.0 and the weapon cylinder touching a hero body record; it lands at most once per attack (a combo once per animation), and a blocked hit uses the flag up. A hero that dies in the window is not hit again (a hit on a hero in the death state does nothing).
13. Statelessness of the attacks. The swing, lunge, overhead counter and boomerang throw do not steer; their direction is the facing at the start. Only the combo turns (snap to the hero on each animation's gate tick), so a hero who circles behind during a swing is missed.
14. Travel is root motion only, confined by the walk mesh (SPEC-0056, wall confinement): the roll (10.7 units sideways) and the lunge (14.3 units forward) are clipped at the edge of the mesh, never carried over it.
15. Flame volley. The ring hit step runs on every pass in every state, so a ring still hurts while the King flinches, blocks or dies (until the script holds it). A ring hits once per volley per ring object. A flinch ends the volley's state (the remaining waves are lost, the flame counter is not reset, so a new volley can start at once if the other gates pass).
16. Boomerang. Ready flag 0 closes the boomerang, flame, swing, lunge and combo selectors and the block and jump choosers; the roll, the movement and the triumph stay open. A flight ends at the hand (catch) or at 600 ticks (ready flag restored, counter not reset). The catch request needs no free state.
17. Phase entries (G10). They set base state 33, freeze everything including rings and the boomerang, and need the arena script to release the King. A drop from 800 straight below 296 (one hit of 505 or more) skips the phase 2 scene.
18. Death. State 20 every tick, story flag 273 once, action 0. The script then plays action 27 in a loop, fades, shows the movie once and removes the entities. Nothing of the controller runs once the script holds the King.
19. Hero dead mid-fight. The King's attacks in progress finish, then the triumph (G3). With a partner hero alive, the swap happens during the triumph and the partner finds the King in triumph (hittable, not guarding) for the rest of its 237 ticks.
20. Out-of-reach hero. Nothing King-specific: it walks while the hero is beyond 25, orbits inside 10 and otherwise idles; a hero on an unreachable platform leaves it walking to the edge of the mesh nearest the route-table path, and in range of nothing but the boomerang (15 to 40) and, in phases 2 and 3, the flame rings (10 to 40, centred on the King, so they miss anyone far above their height).

### G15. Phase speed: R = 1.0, 1.2 and 1.4 (corrects the earlier reading that the speed words reach nothing)

- Rule (High). The controller keeps one speed word. It starts at 1.0, the phase 2 entry stores 1.2 in it and the phase 3 entry stores 1.4 (a drop from phase 3 back to the phase 2 band stores 1.2 again); nothing else writes it. The top of every controller pass copies it into the per-tick speed scale P, and the King's play rate is R = P (its character speed scale is 1.0). The word keeps its value through a hold. While the controller is not running the shared update sets P to 1.0 on every tick, so the clips the arena script plays on the King (the scenes and the intro) always run at R = 1. The first controller pass after a release already uses the phase's word.
- What scales. Every clip advances 0.25 R key per tick, so each clip's tick count, each hit window, each animation event tick and the root travel per tick scale with R. A one-shot clip's total travel is unchanged (it takes fewer ticks). What does not scale: cooldown counters (they count passes), chances, the rolls, the flame wait W (ticks), the boomerang's flight speed, the jump's flight velocity, the ring growth (an effect with its own clock) and the reaction trigger.

### A4. Script timeline (all tick counts are 60 Hz; "I" is the tick the intro starts)

1. Entrance (contact with the entrance sphere, level state 0 clear). Level state 0 is set. The hero starts a walk to locator 10 (not waited for; the walk ends in the free state). The queued movie blocks the whole main loop until it ends, so nothing else advances during it.
2. Bind. The King (and the nearest Bashers, up to the cap of 6) are bound by the first streaming pass after the hero is placed; its counters start at 0 within a tick of the movie's end.
3. After the movie. The hero stands at locator 11 in the free state with the screen still black. The first contact fire of the arena sphere finds all of level states 2 to 7 and flag 273 clear and movie 41 played, and sets level state 2. The next run is the intro.
4. Intro (I = its first tick). 5. Fight. The King runs as described in G1 to G14. Its attack counters read about 120 at the release (G10), so no attack of its own comes in the first 360 ticks; the Bashers act on their own notice rolls. The hero has the normal follow camera and the King as lock-on target.

### A6. Death and retry

- The pack has no death callback and the arena script handles no death, so the engine rules apply (SPEC-0055). A hero at 0 health plays its death clip (about 63 ticks); then the partner takes over if the swap mode allows, else the game-over page follows after 91 more ticks. The swap mode on arrival is whatever the story left: free (mode 2) in the usual Act 3 state, or locked to Tor (mode 0) if the earlier burrows visit locked it (SPEC-0039 module 1244); the arena module sets it only at the end, to 2. With mode 0 and Tor selected, Tor's death is game over; with mode 2 Roberta takes over at Tor's spot (the King triumphs for its 237 ticks while it stands there); both heroes dead is game over.
- Game over with a save or load made earlier in the session: Continue re-applies that snapshot and reloads every pack. Everything in the arena starts again from the disc state: the King at 800 of 800 in phase 1 with a fresh controller, all counters 0 and R = 1.0; all eight Bashers alive (kill bits are not kept across a load); level states and all module words reset; the fence off, objects 5 and 3 on; the heroes at the saved position (state 59), with the saved health. Movie 41 plays again when the entrance is crossed unless the snapshot was taken after it played. Flag 273 is clear unless the snapshot already holds it. There is no checkpoint inside the arena (no save point nearer than about 300 units), so the retry always starts from the player's last save and the whole approach.
- With no save or load pending, game over leads to the title screen.
- What persists across a death: only the snapshot's flags, items, money and the movie played words. Nothing the fight changed (King health, phases, level states) persists.

### A8. What a port most likely gets wrong in this arena

1. Phase speed: the King runs at R = 1.2 and 1.4 in phases 2 and 3 (G15), not at 1.0.
2. Ground versus walk mesh: the floor is at y = 0.305 and the bank rises to 4.9, while the walk mesh is flat at 5.29 and only limits x and z; the King's walls are the mesh edge, the hero's are the fence (after the intro) and the pillars.
3. The Bashers cannot leave their pads; the arena is one King plus a rim fight, not a free-for-all. They keep acting during the scenes, including the hero's frozen seconds.
4. The scenes: both fades out last 0.7 s; the hero is free during the first fade; phase 3 frees the King 60 ticks before the script's release; the phase 2 line waits for the player.
5. The movie blocks the whole loop, the King's counters start at about the movie's end, and the first attack is about 360 ticks after the intro; the intro removes and re-adds Basher 21.
6. Death scene order: pant loop, fade, movie, then removal and the objects; the hero is not held during the pant and the fade.
7. Locator 10 lies outside the entrance sphere, and every fade in this module is to or from black (Medium-High: the module's fade colour word is zero).

## Reach and distances (boss reach pass)

How the figures are made (Medium): the King's skeleton is posed from each attack clip at the cursor of the previous animation pass. The weapon of the shared reach test (Hit test and blocking) is placed from attachment frame 0 (the hand point, as for the hero's own reach routine): A is its anchor and D1 its third axis, and the static cylinder has radius 2.5 and length 7.0 running from A - 1.5 D1 to A + 5.5 D1 (the finite-cylinder base convention of SPEC-0018), as the first shape of the exact rule of SPEC-0018 (round weapon, bone card; High for the rule; the exact test moved the edges by at most 0.4 and a few first ticks by 1). It is tested on each tick with bit 0x1 against Tor's idle bone capsules, Tor level with the King, facing it at horizontal distance d along the King's facing at the request tick T; root travel is included, there is no turning (none of these attacks steers). The secondary swept triangle is left out; it can only add contact between two window ticks.

## Player summary

- Sword swings, Roberta's staff and shots, and Motes all hurt him (800 health, no armour) whenever he is not flinching, catching, rolling, jumping or held. Statuses from Motes work too.
- He guards sometimes (25, 35 and 75 percent of your attacks by phase). A guarding King blocks swings and quick shots that come from the front; attack from the side or behind, or use a Mote power step, a Mote attack or a full-release shot, which ignore the guard.
- Each hit makes him flinch, so he cannot be chain-hit; time the next swing after the flinch.
- Avoid his boomerang and flame rings (a shield does not stop them; keep your feet above or off the ring band) and stand outside the reach of his swing, lunge and combo; a shield held facing him stops those melee hits. The fight gets faster in phases 2 and 3 (below 75 and 37 percent health).
- In the Gnarfas arena he is only a bystander and cannot be fought.

## Where it lives on the disc

- The boss's tuning values are 32-bit words in the boot program's initialized data. The game reads them at runtime by virtual address, so a clean build should read the shipped defaults from the disc and not hard-code them. The boss's own values sit at data addresses 0x2DB0C8 to 0x2DB1BC. Shared constants that the boss uses sit at 0x2DB1C0 to 0x2DB1D0 (see Layout).
- "int" is a signed 32-bit integer and "float" an IEEE single. Percent values are whole numbers from 0 to 100 unless stated.
- The hero is the player's character. Distances are measured horizontally from the boss to the hero (or to the boss's home point), in world units (see Distances), except the melee reach test, which is three-dimensional. The unit scale is not confirmed (see Open questions).
- Animation numbers are entries of the boss's animation table (SPEC-0005).

## Distances

Every distance from the boss to the hero, or from the boss to its home point, is measured in the horizontal plane: sqrt(dx^2 + dz^2). Height is ignored. Unit directions used for facing and steering are horizontal (except the boomerang's own flight, which is three-dimensional). High. The one exception is the melee reach test of the shared hit: its distance gate uses the full three-dimensional length (see Hit test and blocking).

## Behavior

### Controller start (first run only) (Confidence: High for the order; Medium-High for the lookup parameter)

The boss's controller runs once per tick. Its first run does this, in order, before the first tick of behavior:

1. Navigation mesh (SPEC-0018 describes the navigation mesh). Read the boss's current mesh. If none is attached, print a diagnostic line. Then search the level's mesh records, in order, for the first record that passes the record's flag test and has an entry that contains the boss's current position. The search runs over the walk-mesh list of the boss's owning world (the world set on the character when it is placed; when none is set, the current world), and takes a mesh of the walking kind (Medium-High). If a record is found, attach to it: the boss's current mesh becomes that record, the boss's position becomes the attach point, and a success line is printed. If none is found, print a failure line and do not attach. The search is not repeated on later ticks.
2. Locomotion slots. Mode 0, 1 and 2 play animation 2; mode 3 and 4 play none; mode 5 (orbit right) plays animation 3; mode 6 (orbit left) plays animation 4.
3. Blackboard defaults: state idle, sub-animation 0, ready flag 1, big-laugh request word 0, catch request word 0, phase 1 (phase field 0), flame-wave hesitation counter -1, speed 1.0, home point = the boss's start position, jump ramp bounds 15 and 28 units, and an empty flame-projectile pool (five records, all inactive).

### Activation in play: what makes the King run (Confidence: High for the engine rules; Medium-High for the tick timeline)

The King has no activation switch of its own. Nothing in its placement, in the level scripts or in the host calls turns the fight on; it runs because the character streaming of the area binds it, and the scripts only hold it and release it around the cutscenes.

- **Placement (High).** In pack 1246 the King is record 16 of the embedded character block (entity id 16, the entity table lists the 26 embedded records in order): type 14, variant 0, kind key 2325, position (731.1, 5.3, -2124.9), default AI mode word 0, behavior class 32 through its monster row. The record has no start-state, active or hidden field: every byte outside the documented fields (SPEC-0006, Kind 3) is the same fill as in the arena's other records. The only differences from the Werelupe Bashers (records 17 to 24) are the position and facing, the model indexes and the mode word (0 against 7).
- **Binding is the activation (High).** A record becomes a live character when the block's streaming pass binds it (SPEC-0006, Character activation, streaming and kill memory): the activation filter admits it (pack 1246 registers none), its kill bit is clear (clear on a new game), it is not bound yet, it lies within 280 units (squared 3D distance below 78,400) of the active hero, and it is among the nearest eligible records up to the block's cap. The cap is 6 until SetMaxActiveAI changes it; the nearest unbound record replaces the farthest bound one when its squared distance plus 20 is smaller, at most one swap per tick. A bind resets the character and sets base state 1 (free). In base state 1 the class coroutine of this spec runs every tick, so the controller start and the tick order above begin on the tick of the bind.
- **Held states (High).** The class coroutine does not run while the character is in base state 33 (script hold), 16, 25, 36, 42, 53 or 55. GrabEntityControl sets 33, so a grabbed King does nothing at all; ReleaseEntityControl, MoveEntity with control 1 and the end of PlayEntityAnim with control 1 set state 1 again (SPEC-0030).
- **Calls that do not activate (High).** BossFightTweakHero(14, 0) only refills the heroes' health and second meter (the instance number picks a one-hero or two-hero refill and nothing else; it names no entity). SetMaxActiveAI(12) raises the embedded block's cap from 6 to 12, which lets more of the arena's enemies bind; it does not bind the King. ActivateEntity is called only for entity 21, which the intro first removed with DeactivateEntity(21); the King is never forced. The module's data holds no constructor list and no callback table (a second registration on a trigger would replace the first, but the module registers each of its two triggers once, from Init).
- **Attack timers (High).** All cooldown counters start at 0 when the controller starts and rise by 1 every tick, and an attack whose counter is not above its cooldown cannot start. The shortest cooldowns are 8 s (boomerang, lunge) = 480 ticks, so the King cannot start any of its own attacks in the first 481 ticks after the bind. The reaction attacks (block, jump, roll) need the hero to swing. Before that the King can only turn, walk (hero farther than 25 units), hop back or orbit (hero closer than 10 units).

**Worked timeline, pack 1246 (Medium-High; ticks are counted from the tick T on which the entrance script moves the hero to locator 11).** Distances are squared 3D distances from the hero at locator 11 (752.0, 5.1, -2135.3): Basher 21 = 196, Basher 17 = 416, the King = 545, Basher 20 = 775, Basher 18 = 1,244, Basher 22 = 1,697, Basher 19 = 2,124, Basher 23 = 2,476, Basher 24 = 2,645, Swamp Tentacle 15 = 9,625, tentacle 14 = 10,225, tentacle 13 = 17,011.

The six bound records are then tentacle 15, Basher 21, tentacle 14, tentacle 13, Basher 20 and Basher 17 (squared distances 1,497, 5,576, 6,840, 6,971, 7,475 and 8,173); the King (12,334) is the seventh and is not bound.
2. T. The entrance script moves the hero to locator 11, sleeps one tick, refills the heroes (BossFightTweakHero(14, 0)) and plays movie 41 the first time.
3. T + 1 (first streaming pass). The King (545) is the nearest unbound record; the farthest bound one is tentacle 13 (17,011); 545 + 20 is below that, so the King replaces it. It is bound in base state 1 and its controller starts (navigation lookup, defaults; counters at 0). The next two ticks bind Basher 18 (replacing tentacle 14) and Basher 22 (replacing tentacle 15), giving the six nearest: 21, 17, 16, 20, 18, 22.
4. After the movie. The hero's contact with the arena sphere fires trigger 0 each tick. The first run that sees movie 41 played sets level state 2; the next run is the intro.

The King is bound by then (any order of the tick's steps is fine, because the bind happened at T + 1). 8. Death. State 20 (terminal) sets flag 273. The next trigger run takes the flag-273 branch, which is the whole death scene: it loops action 27 (cue 17) on the still-present King for 1.3 s, fades out for 1.0 s, plays movie 42 the first time, sets level state 8, and only then removes entities 16 to 24 with DeactivateEntity, which sets their kill bits and releases them. **The King with a standing hero (High).** What releases the King after the intro is the arena script's release call in the intro branch, the last step before level state 3 is set (about 357 ticks after the intro starts); no other trigger or script step is needed, and the hero does not have to move. The intro is started by the arena sphere's contact trigger, which fires on every tick the hero's contact volume overlaps the sphere, whether or not he moves. The intro walks the hero to locator 8 (747.1, 0.3, -2132.9), 10.65 horizontal units from locator 7 (737.4, 0.3, -2128.5) where the intro put the King, and the intro clip (action 0x12, 60 keys, one shot) has no root travel, so the King stays on locator 7. That spot is a dead zone of the King's rules: the hero is farther than 10 (no swing, lunge, combo or orbit), nearer than 25 (no walk), farther than 5 (no hop back or jump), nearer than 15 (no boomerang, which needs a distance between 15 and 40), the flame wave has chance 0 in phase 1, and block, jump and roll start only on the rising edge of a hero swing. A released King at full health with a motionless hero on locator 8 therefore stays in state 0 (idle) for as long as nothing changes.
- Level states and the King: level state 0 alone (entrance, fade, movie 41): the King is not bound until the hero is placed at locator 11, then it runs freely but cannot attack for 481 ticks. Level state 2 set and 3 clear (intro): the King is on locator 7 and is held (base state 33) while action 0x12 plays, then runs freely until the release. Level state 3: released, the controller runs under its normal rules (this is the state of a standing-hero trace with level states 0, 2 and 3 up). Level states 4 and 6 are raised by the King's own phase entries and hold it until the scene's release; 5 and 7 are set at the end of those scenes; 8 follows the death scene.
- Test vector (phase 1, full health, King on locator 7, hero not swinging, counters above 480): hero at 10.65: idle on every tick. Hero at 9.9: an orbit starts (side coin) and swing, lunge or combo rolls begin. Hero at 15.5: boomerang roll each tick at 90 percent. Hero at 25.5: walk. Hero at 40.0 exactly: boomerang does not start (strict) but the walk does.

**Pack 985 (Gnarfas and the King in the same area; Medium-High).** The embedded block of pack 985 has nine records: 0 to 4 Gelert Assassins (all at one point), 5 and 6 Illusen (one point), 7 Gnarfas (-617.4, 126.2, -558.8) and 8 the Werelupe King (-580.8, 121.9, -496.1, beside Illusen's point). - Flag 281 set (the Gelert Assassin fight, SPEC-0013): the filter admits ordinals 0 to 4 and 6 and refuses 5, 7 and 8. Asking about an ordinal below 5 assigns walk mesh 0 to that entity.
So with flag 281 clear the streaming pass binds Illusen (5), Gnarfas (7) and the King (8) together on the first pass after the hero is placed at locator 9 (-637.0, 122.7, -567.2): they lie 21, 92 and 91 units away, within 280 units, and three records are far below the cap of 6 (this module never calls SetMaxActiveAI). Gnarfas's controller runs from its bind (SPEC-0020). The module's control thread then, one tick after it sees the arrival word (the sleep comes first, so the region pass of that next tick has already bound the three records), moves entity 7 to position 11, grabs entity 8 and plays its animation 0 (the idle loop, with no wait and control option 0, so nothing ever releases him); nothing in the branch releases entity 8, and the defeat sequence deactivates entities 7 and 8 on the tick Gnarfas's death event is seen. So the King in pack 985 is bound but held in state 33 for the whole fight, standing in his idle loop and watching: his controller runs at most the single pass of his bind tick (which cannot start an attack: every cooldown counter starts at 0 and the shortest cooldown is 481 ticks) and never again, and he cannot be hit. ### Each tick, in order (Confidence: High for the order; Medium-High for the detail of the pre-check)

Each later tick does the following in order:

1. The class loop clears the boss's unhittable word (+0x7F0) to 0, sets base state 1 (the states that guard or hold set their own value later in the tick), and copies the controller's speed word into the boss's per-tick speed scale P. The word starts at 1.0; the phase 2 handler stores 1.2 in it and the phase 3 handler stores 1.4, and nothing else writes it, so P is 1.0, 1.2 or 1.4 by phase (see Edge cases and G15).
2. Pre-check. The steps below run in this order. A later step can replace the result of an earlier one in the same tick.
   a. Hit flinch. If the boss's reaction word is "hit" (value 10): clear the word (the shared AI-mode setter called with mode 0, which only writes 0 to the word and changes nothing else), go to hit flinch, and pick flinch animation 0x0B, 0x0C or 0x0D with probability 1/3 each. This step has no state gate.
   b. Catch. If the catch request word is 1: clear it and go to catch (animation 0x14).
   c. Big laugh. If the big-laugh request word is 1 and the state is free: go to big laugh. The word starts at 0 and is only ever cleared, so this never happens (see Edge cases).
   d. Triumph. If the hero is down and the state is free and the state is not already triumph: go to triumph (animation 0x12).
   e. Health. Let f = current health / maximum health. If f <= 0: go to death, every tick. Else if f < 0.37 and the phase is not 3 and the state is not already the phase 3 entry: go to the phase 3 entry. Else if f < 0.75 and the phase is not 2 and the state is not already the phase 2 entry: go to the phase 2 entry. See Phases for the phase-2 test.
3. Reaction (on the tick the hero's reaction trigger rises only; see Attack selection). If the state is free, run the block choice. Then, only if the state is still free, the jump choice. Then, only if the state is still free, the roll choice.
4. Attacks, in this order: boomerang, flame wave, swing, lunge, combo. Each runs only when its gate allows (see Attack selection).
5. Movement: the jump chooser runs first (every tick, with the same conditions as on the reaction edge; see Attack selection, Jump), then the walk, hop back and orbit choices (see Movement).
6. Run the current state's behavior (see States).
7. Update the boomerang flight and the flame-wave projectiles (see Boomerang flight and Flame-wave volley). A debug text routine also runs each tick; it has no gameplay effect, is not specified here, and its text is never shown (Open question 10). Then wait one tick.

A later choice in the same tick sees the state set by an earlier one, so an earlier choice blocks the later ones.

### Hit flinch (any state)

When the hero's attack damages the boss, the shared monster hit handler applies the damage and sets the boss's generic "was hit" mode (the reaction word). On the next tick the boss enters the hit-flinch state, whatever it was doing (the flinch has no state gate). It plays one of the flinch animations 0x0B, 0x0C or 0x0D, each with probability 1/3, and returns to idle when the animation ends (gate 37, 45 or 41 ticks after the entry tick). The flinch handler sets the unhittable word every tick from the entry tick through the exit tick, so the hero cannot hit the boss again until the tick after the exit (Hurt side). The flinch is a free state: an attack, block, jump or roll whose gates pass on any tick of it replaces it at once, and then the unhittable word is no longer set. The catch, big laugh, triumph and phase and death checks of the same tick run after the flinch and can replace it. A flinch can interrupt a jump, a volley or an orbit.

### Triumph

While the hero is down (knocked out), the boss, if its state is free, enters the triumph state and plays animation 0x12 once. It returns to idle when the animation ends. While the triumph is running it is not restarted. If the hero is still down on the next tick after the triumph has ended, the triumph starts again.

### Phases and health thresholds (Confidence: High)

Let f = current health / maximum health, as a floating-point number. The boss starts in phase 1. The phase is kept in one phase field of the controller record with the values 0, 1 and 2 for phases 1, 2 and 3: it is 0 at start, the phase-2 entry writes 1 and the phase-3 entry writes 2. The "phase is not 3" and "phase is not 2" tests below read this field (values 2 and 1), and the flame-wave chance index is the same field (High).

- If f <= 0: the boss enters the death state. This check runs every tick before the state is run, even while another state is active.
- Else if f < 0.37 and the boss is not in phase 3 and is not already entering phase 3: it enters the phase 3 entry.
- Else if f < 0.75 and the boss is not in phase 2 and is not already entering phase 2: it enters the phase 2 entry.

The intended rule is that phases only go up: 1, then 2, then 3. The shipped phase-2 test does not check for phase 3, though. A boss in phase 3 whose health is at least 0.37 and below 0.75 enters the phase 2 entry, and its phase becomes 2 again (phase-2 chances). It returns to phase 3 only when health falls below 0.37. This is a reproducible quirk (see Edge cases). Whether health can rise during play is not established.

Phase 2 entry: the boss sets its phase to 2, stores 1.2 in its speed word (the source of P from the next pass on; G15), and sends the phase-change event 4 (level state 4). It requests its idle animation and is idle by the end of the same tick, so the entry takes one tick and has no animation wait. Phase 3 entry: the boss sets its phase to 3, stores 1.4 in its speed word, sends the phase-change event 6 (level state 6), and is idle by the end of the same tick. Each entry also sets the boss's base state to 33 (script hold) in the pre-check, before the handler runs. The controller does not run in base state 33 (Activation in play, Held states), so from the next tick the boss is frozen and not hittable until the arena script releases it (pack 1246: Fight AI build guide, G10). Counters, the ready flag and the other controller words keep their values through the hold. A drop from above 75 percent straight below 37 percent enters phase 3 directly, so the phase 2 entry is skipped (High).

### Attack selection (Confidence: High for the order, gates, distances and chances)

Each attack choice has the same steps:

1. Cooldown. The attack has a counter. Each tick, whatever the state, the choice first compares the counter with the cooldown in ticks (cooldown seconds x 60, truncated: 480 or 600). While the counter is at or below that value, it adds one and the choice does nothing else. Once the counter is above that value (481 or 601; it never rises further), the choice goes on to its state, ready-flag, distance and roll tests on every tick until it starts the attack. The first such tick is run 482 (cooldown 8 s) or run 602 (cooldown 10 s) counted from a counter of 0 on run 1, and E + 482 or E + 602 after a reset on tick E. The chance is a per-tick chance. The counter resets when the attack starts (swing, lunge, combo), when the catch ends (boomerang) and when the flame volley ends (flame wave). The swing counter also resets on the tick the swing state ends, so its gap is counted from the swing's end (Fight AI build guide, G6).
2. State gate. A new attack can start only while the state gate is open. The gate is open in idle, hit flinch, walk, hop back, orbit right and orbit left. It is closed in catch, big laugh, triumph, the two phase entries, block, jump, roll, every attack state (swing, overhead counter, boomerang throw, flame wave, lunge, combo), and death.
3. Ready flag. Boomerang, flame wave, swing, lunge and combo require the ready flag to be 1. Block and jump also require it. Roll does not.
4. Distance window. The hero's distance must satisfy the window for that attack.
5. Roll. Compare the roll with the chance for the current phase (see Roll and coin rules). The attack starts if the roll is less than the chance.

The rules for each attack:

- **Block.** Runs on the hero's reaction rising edge. Needs the ready flag and the state gate. Starts if the roll is less than the block chance for the phase (25 / 35 / 75).
- **Jump.** Runs on the same rising edge and also on every tick, at the start of the movement choice (so it is a standing return-home rule, see Movement). Needs the ready flag, the state gate and a hero distance below 5. A second distance, measured from the boss's home point (the position it had at the controller's start) to the boss, must be above 15 units. The start chance then rises in a straight line: c = 5 + (d - 15) / 13 x 75 percent, where d is that home distance. There is no cap: from d = 31.47 units on, every roll succeeds. The jump starts if the roll is less than c.
- **Roll.** Runs on the same rising edge. Needs the state gate. Starts if the roll is less than 50. A coin then picks roll animation 9 (coin 0) or 10 (coin 1).
- **Boomerang.** Needs the ready flag, the state gate and a hero distance strictly between 15 and 40. Cooldown 8 s. Starts if the roll is less than 90 / 50 / 25.
- **Flame wave.** Needs the ready flag, the state gate and a hero distance strictly between 10 and 40. Cooldown 10 s. Starts if the roll is less than 0 / 50 / 75. In phase 1 the chance is 0, so flame waves never start there. The three chances are indexed by the phase field 0, 1 or 2 (phases 1, 2 and 3; High, see Phases); a value outside 0 to 2 never starts a wave. The trigger's counter adds one only while it is at most 600, so it stops at 601, and a failed check does not reset it; once the gates pass, a roll is made each tick. On a start the boss enters state 13 and plays animation 0x1A, the wave index is set to 0, the fired flag of the wave is cleared, and six words are cleared: the five per-ring hit flags (one per flame object) and one jump word that the jump sets again before it is used, so that clear has no effect (High). The main cooldown counter is not reset by the start. Both distance ends are strict: exactly 10 or exactly 40 units does not start a wave. The trigger's gate word is the ready flag (High), so no flame wave can start while a boomerang is out.
- **Swing.** Needs the ready flag, the state gate and a hero distance below 10. Cooldown 10 s. Starts if the roll is less than 60 / 46 / 20.
- **Lunge.** Needs the ready flag, the state gate and a hero distance below 10. Cooldown 8 s. Starts if the roll is less than 30 / 40 / 50.
- **Combo.** Needs the ready flag, the state gate and a hero distance below 10. Cooldown 10 s. Starts if the roll is less than 10 / 20 / 30.
- **Overhead counter.** Decided inside the block state (see the block row of the States table), not after the recovery animation. It runs on each block tick where the one-shot word is not set and the hero's reaction trigger is false. It starts only if the roll is less than 25, the hero is closer than 5 units and the hero's attack sub-state word (+0xF90 of the hero) equals 13 (not "action state 13": sub-state 13 is Tor's recoil after his swing was blocked, set by the hero's blocked-swing hook for sub-states 2 to 4, 6, 11 and 12 of Tor only, and Roberta's landing after her leap; SPEC-0036, State 13 arm tables). The roll is tested first. A block is held while the reaction trigger stays true, so a counter cannot start during a held block. Because a blocked swing also sets the one-shot blocked word, which the block state reads before this decision, the recovery branch wins after a blocked Tor swing; the overhead counter therefore needs the hero in sub-state 13 without a pending blocked word (Medium; rare in practice).
- **Roll counter-attack.** Follows a roll. Starts if the roll has finished, the hero is closer than 5 units, and the roll is at most 100 (so it always starts with the shipped chance; see Edge cases). It enters the swing state with animation 0x16 and sets the swing toggle to 0, so the next ordinary swing plays 0x17.

Reaction trigger: the block, jump and roll choices run only on the tick the hero's reaction trigger rises from false to true. The trigger is the hero attack-state test (SPEC-0040, Behavior 18): Tor in action state 13 with attack sub-state 2 to 12, or Tor in the plunge attack of the jump states; Roberta only in action state 13 with sub-state 5 or 12 (her ordinary shots, spins and leap never start a reaction) (High). None of the three choices has a King-to-hero distance test except the jump (hero closer than 5): a swing from any distance can start a block or a roll. It is cleared when it goes false again. The rising edge is recorded on the first tick the trigger is true, even if the boss is busy at that moment. A swing that starts while the boss is busy is therefore missed, and no block, jump or roll follows it.

Ready flag: it starts at 1. It is set to 0 only at the boomerang release. It is set back to 1 when the catch ends, or when the boomerang flight times out (600 ticks). No other path sets it.

### Movement (Confidence: High for thresholds, the orbit end rule and the speeds at R = 1)

Each tick, after the attacks, the movement choice first runs the jump chooser (Attack selection, Jump: ready flag, free state, hero closer than 5, boss farther than 15 from home, chance rising from 5 to 80 percent over home distances 15 to 28 and beyond). If the jump starts, the state is no longer free and the rest of the movement choice does nothing. Then, when the state is free and the boss is not flinching:

- If the hero is farther than 25 units: the boss walks toward the hero (locomotion mode 0, animation 2). The walk lasts until the hero is closer than 25 units.
- Otherwise, a hop timer adds one each tick on which this step is reached (free state, not the flinch, hero within 25 units), before anything else is tested. If the hero is closer than 5 units, the boss is not ready, and the timer is then above 60, the timer is cleared and a roll of less than 50 starts a hop back (animation 5); a failed roll still falls through to the orbit choice below. Because the hop back is a free state and the hero is closer than 10 on the next tick, the next tick's choice replaces it with an orbit (hop back lasts one tick). Hop-back quirks are listed under Edge cases.
- Otherwise, if the hero is closer than 10 units and the boss is not already orbiting, a fair coin picks the right or left orbit (locomotion modes 5 and 6). If the boss is already orbiting, the choice does nothing, so the orbit continues.

The speeds of these movements come from the root motion of their clips (see Travel).

Orbit end rule. The orbit has no timer and no exit of its own. While the hero is between 10 and 25 units away, an orbit that is running continues indefinitely. An orbit ends only when one of these happens: the orbit is blocked (jump), the hero moves beyond 25 units (walk starts), the hop-back test passes, an attack starts, the boss is hit (flinch), or a reaction edge starts block, jump or roll.

### Orbit side (Confidence: High)

The side vector is the unit vector of the horizontal hero direction u crossed with the reference vector. The reference vector is the constant up vector (0, 1, 0, 0) at 0x3932E0, written at boot (SPEC-0007). So the side is s = normalize(u x up) = (-u.z, 0, u.x). The orbit target is the boss's position plus 5 units times the side vector (right orbit), or minus that (left orbit). The orbit is re-aimed every tick, and the boss turns toward its motion as it strafes.

### Boomerang flight (Confidence: High for the target order, steering and catch request; Medium for the frame details)

- Release: throw action 0x13 releases on its key-7 event. The controller reads animation events before that tick's animation pass, so the event uses the preceding pass's sampled pose. The release is request-relative E + 28 at R = 1.0, E + 24 at R = 1.2, and E + 20 at R = 1.4; the clip cursor at those reads is 7.0, 7.2 and 7.0 respectively (SPEC-0005, Playback). The boomerang starts from its current held logical position and moves along the unit vector of (0.5 x forward - 0.5 x side) of the boss. The ready flag is set to 0. The flight timeout counter is set to 600 ticks. Its first movement and hit check run in the same controller pass as release, with the hit check after the 0.7-unit movement.
- Hand frame: the King uses attachment point 0 of his own model set (model set 0 of his region, 51 bones; his record names that set). That point is on bone 24, the end of the arm chain (shoulder bone 8, then bones 21 to 24), with zero rotation and the local offset (-0.614, -0.238, 0). Let T0 and Z0 be that point's origin and third basis in world space, from the posed bone 24 and the offset. The normal held model is drawn at T0. The controller's held logical position is H = T0 + 1.1 Z0; its inactive and timeout position uses H. In the King's own frame (x sideways, y up, z forward, origin at his placement), the pose at throw key 7 (clip 6 of his main set, cursor 7.0, used at R = 1 and R = 1.4) gives T0 approximately (-2.80, 4.15, 0.70), Z0 approximately (0.60, 0.77, 0.20) and H approximately (-2.13, 5.00, 0.91). At R = 1.2 the release reads cursor 7.2, which gives T0 approximately (-2.25, 4.20, 2.13), Z0 approximately (0.73, 0.68, 0.06) and H approximately (-1.45, 4.95, 2.20); the hand moves about 0.5 to 1.2 units per tenth of a key in this part of the throw, so these numbers are sensitive to the exact cursor (Medium). The held position is therefore about 5.0 above the King's feet at release (4.95 at R = 1.2), and waypoint A, which is built from T0, is about 4.2 above his feet when it is computed (the return target follows the hand as it moves). Apply the character's current world transform (turning about the vertical axis, plus his position) to these local points; the height above his feet does not change when he turns. The 73-bone model set of the same region, whose attach point 0 is its root bone, is Gnarfas's and must not be used for the King.
- Let D be the horizontal hero distance and u the horizontal unit direction from the boss to the hero. Let s be the same side vector as the orbit, s = (-u.z, 0, u.x) (see Orbit side; High). Waypoint A = T0 + 0.5 D u + 0.4 D s. Waypoint B = T0 + 0.5 D u - 0.4 D s, computed when the hero point is reached.
- Targets in order: waypoint A; then the hero point (the hero's x and z, height = the ground under the hero + 2, re-aimed every tick); then waypoint B; then T0 (re-aimed every tick).
- Each tick: compute the three-dimensional offset to the current target, its length r and its unit direction t. If r < 0.5 the boomerang snaps to the target and that target counts as reached. Otherwise k = 1.0 if r < 5, 0.25 if r < 10, else 0.1 (the far steering weight). The direction becomes the normalized (1 - k) x direction + k x t. The position moves by 0.7 units times the direction, once per tick (boomerang speed).
- If the position is below ground + 1.0 it is raised to ground + 1.0 and the downward direction component is set to 0.
- Reaching the hand target sets the catch request. The boss enters the catch on the next tick, because the pre-check runs before dispatch (animation 0x14).
- **Throw end**: the boss's throw state ends when its own animation ends. It does not wait for the boomerang. So the boss can be idle, and even start other choices, while the boomerang is still in the air. The ready flag stays 0 until the catch or the timeout.
- **Hit (High)**: each tick while it flies, if the boomerang's position is at most 65.0 units from the hero (the same gate as the melee reach test), the boomerang is tested against each record of the hero body list (see Hero body shape records) with length above 0. The boomerang's test shape is a finite cylinder of radius 2.5 units and length 0.25 units, centred on the boomerang's position, with the boomerang's stored direction as its axis. The test is static only; no sweep runs. On the first record it overlaps, the boomerang's hit flag is set to 1, the hero takes the boomerang's damage (5, raw: unblockable and not reduced by the hero's defences), and the test ends. The flight step calls this hit only while the hit flag is 0, and the next boomerang release clears the flag, so the boomerang hurts at most once per throw (High).
- While held, its logical position is H = T0 + 1.1 Z0. Its ordinary attached model draw is at T0, so the displayed held model origin and logical projectile position differ by 1.1 units along Z0 (High).
- The thrown model frame is rebuilt from the boomerang's current flight direction and a reference axis, so its orientation follows the flight path (High). The reference axis starts from the held frame's second basis direction. During waypoint phases 0–2 it eases 95% from its previous value toward world up; during phases 3–4 its target changes to the negative of the hand frame's first basis direction. In phase 4 it snaps to that target. The 0.6-radian-per-tick spin rotates the model about its flight-direction axis; the eased vector supplies the frame reference used to orient the perpendicular axes (Medium-High).
- Spin and speed are applied per tick, not per second (Medium-High).
- Timeout: the 600-tick flight limit stops waypoint updates. The boomerang is placed at its held-in-hand transform, without a catch animation. Its waypoint phase field is not changed to a timeout phase; it retains the phase active when the limit expires. The ready flag is restored, but the boomerang counter is not reset.

### Jump flight (Confidence: High)

- Launch (animation 6): it turns with weight 0.2 per tick. On the tick the launch clip ends, the velocity becomes v = (dx / 60, 60 x 0.05 x 0.5, dz / 60) = (dx / 60, 1.5, dz / 60), where (dx, dz) is the horizontal offset from the boss to home (60.0 is the tick-rate word 0x2DB1C0, 0.05 the jump step word 0x2DB1C4). Clip 7 starts.
- Air (animation 7): each later air tick, if the three-dimensional distance to home is at least 3.0: v.y = v.y - 0.05, then the position is moved by v directly (not through physics). It turns with weight 0.2 per tick. Otherwise the landing clip 8 starts.
- Landing (animation 8): the landing does not move the boss and it does not turn. The state ends when clip 8 ends (see the Jump row).
- Home is the boss's position when its controller started.
- Gravity during the jump (High for the flag writes, Medium-High for the effect): the jump state sets the body's no-gravity flag (the flag of SPEC-0040 Behavior 14A) at the start of each of its ticks. During launch and air it stays set, so the body's own gravity does not pull the boss down and only the jump's own v moves it. On every tick on which clip 8 is the current animation, the state clears the flag again, so for the whole landing clip the body's normal character gravity and ground contact act: a boss that ends its flight above the ground falls under normal gravity (vertical speed starting from 0) and is held on the ground once it reaches it. On the first landing tick (the tick that requests clip 8) the flag is still set. After the jump the flag stays clear.

### Combo (Confidence: High for sequence, hit count and flag persistence)

The combo has three attack animations, played in order: 0x16, then 0x17, then 0x18. Each of the three hits goes through the shared once-per-attack hit (see below), with its own damage value: the first hit uses the first combo damage (10), the second the second (10), and the third the third (10). Within each animation the hit runs only while that animation's progress is below 0.99 (see Hit window).

The once flag is cleared when the combo starts (as for every attack the selectors start), and at two transitions:

- When animation 0x16 reaches 0.99 progress, the flag is cleared and the combo moves on to animation 0x17.
- When animation 0x17 reaches 0.99 progress, the flag is cleared and the combo moves on to animation 0x18.

When animation 0x18 reaches 0.99 progress, the sub-animation becomes 1 without a flag clear (animation 1 is requested on the next tick, and its own 0.99 gate, 140 ticks later, returns the boss to idle). So the combo can hit up to three times, at most once in each animation. On the gate tick of each of the three animations the boss turns fully (weight 1.0) to face the hero; it does not steer on other ticks (High). After the third hit it plays animation 1 (the growl, cue 1), then returns to idle. Ticks from the combo's start E: first animation requested E, gate E + 69; second requested E + 70, gate E + 143; third requested E + 144, gate E + 233; growl requested E + 234, idle at E + 374.

### Flame-wave volley (Confidence: High for counts, waits and the ring hit rule; Medium-High for the ring's growth)

The flame wave plays its animation. There are four waves in phases 1 and 2, and five waves in phase 3 (the boss holds five flame objects). The boss turns toward the hero with weight 0.05 per tick during the volley. The fire event is bit 0x400, on frame 12 of the flame-wave clip (0x1A). The volley uses the five flame objects held by the boss itself (not the shared enemy projectile pool).

Per-volley state: the wave index w (0 at the start), the wait counter, and a fired flag (cleared at the start of each wave).

Each tick of the flame-wave state:
1. If the current animation is not the requested sub-animation, request it and do nothing else.
2. If the current animation is 0x1A and its progress has reached 0.99: if w is below the last index (3 in phases 1 and 2, 4 in phase 3), add 1 to w, clear the fired flag, request the idle sub-animation (0) and set the wait counter to a uniform integer from the wait minimum to the wait maximum (0 to 15, both inclusive). Otherwise the volley ends: the state becomes idle, the sub-animation 0, and the flame-wave cooldown counter is reset to 0.
3. Otherwise, if the current animation is not 0x1A (the idle sub-animation between waves): if the wait counter is below 1, request animation 0x1A; else subtract 1 from it.
4. Otherwise (0x1A playing): on the fire event, if the fired flag is clear, start flame object w and set the fired flag.

So between waves the idle animation is current for wait + 2 ticks, and each wave restarts animation 0x1A from its start.

**Flame ring.** Starting flame object w places it at the boss's position at that moment and starts its ring effect. A wave is a ring of flame that spreads out from where the boss stood, not a shot aimed at the hero. The ring has a current radius r and a height h, both read from the running ring effect each tick. The ring stops when its effect ends (High for the start point).

**Ring growth (Medium-High).** Each flame object runs effect 415 of effect bank 3 (SPEC-0034). That effect has one emitter that makes one burst of four identical, motionless particles at the ring centre. The flame object reads its r and h from one of them: r is the particle's first size component and h is its second size component times the flame object's scale (1.0). The particle's size follows the effect system's piecewise size rule (SPEC-0034): it starts at (0.5, 6.2963, 0.5), and on every effect tick (dt = 1/60 s) each component adds rate x dt / L, where L is the particle's life and the rate is picked by the life fraction f = age / L reached on that tick:

- r (first component): rate 24.5 for every f up to 2.0, so r grows linearly and reaches 0.5 + 24.5 = 25.0 at the end of the life.
- h (second component): rate −64.0 while f ≤ 0.05208, then −2.52015 while f ≤ 2.0.

L is 0.8 s with the effect's life jitter of up to ±0.02 s (SPEC-0034), so the ring lives about 48 ticks. With L = 0.8 s, r grows by 0.5104 units per tick (about 30.6 units per second) from 0.5 to 25.0, and h falls from 6.30 to about 3.63 in the first two ticks and then by about 0.0525 per tick to about 1.21 at the end. The hit band (0.6 wide) is wider than the per-tick growth, so a ring always meets a still hero whose distance from the centre lies within the ring's range, on at least one tick.

**Ring hit test.** Every tick, in every state of the boss (it runs in the per-tick update after the state handler), each of the five flame objects that is running is tested against the hero. Let d be the horizontal distance from the ring's centre to the hero's position and dy the hero's height minus the centre's height. The ring touches the hero when r − 0.3 < d < r + 0.3 and dy < h (no lower bound on dy). A touch deals 15 (word 0x2DB198) once per flame object: each object has a hit flag, which is set by the touch and is cleared only when a new volley starts. That damage is raw: it goes straight to the hero's take-hit, is not reduced by the hero's defences, and cannot be blocked (High). So the hero avoids a ring by standing inside it once it has passed, by staying ahead of it, or by being more than h above its centre as it passes.

### Shared once-per-attack hit: swing, overhead counter, lunge and combo (Confidence: High for routing, gates, guard branch, progress test and flag persistence)

One shared routine delivers the damage of the swing, the overhead counter, the lunge and each combo hit. Each attack passes its own damage value (see the table in Layout): swing 10, overhead counter 15, lunge 10, and combo hits 10, 10 and 10. The routine does not take a common damage value.

Each tick the routine runs while its attack is active and its hit window is open. Its inputs are the boss, the hero, the attack's once flag, and the damage for this hit. The checks run in this order, and a check that fails ends this tick without a hit:

1. Once flag. If the once flag is set, nothing happens. A hit that has already landed in this attack therefore blocks all later hits of the same attack, even if the hero stays in reach.
2. Boss predicate. The boss's own action-frame query must be true. The King, like every non-hero character, is an AI-controlled character, so this is the AI-character attack-active query: true when any of animation event bits 0x1 to 0x10 (weapons 1 to 5) is set on the current frame of its playing animations (SPEC-0005; weapon volumes in SPEC-0029). The same query asked of the hero uses the hero's attack-active word instead (SPEC-0036). Confidence: High.
3. Reach test. The hero must pass the reach test (see Hit test and blocking).

When all three checks pass, the hit is resolved and the once flag is set. The flag is set on every hit that passes the reach test, whether or not the hero blocks it. A hit that fails any check does not set the flag, so a later tick in the window may still hit.

Where the flag lives: in the controller's shared state (a word at byte offset 0x60 of that state). The controller keeps that state from one tick to the next, so the flag survives between ticks (High: the state is a local record of the never-returning controller loop; Open question 15).

Clearing: the flag is cleared at the start of each attack that the attack selectors start (swing, overhead counter, lunge, combo) and at the combo's two transitions. It is not cleared when a roll counter-attack starts (see Edge cases).

Hit resolution (guard branch): if the hero is guarding and the dot product of the hero's and the boss's facing directions is at most -0.5, the hit deals no damage: the hero's take-hit runs with 0 and the blocked flag, so the hero plays its block reaction (the block sound cue and hero animation 0x40, SPEC-0029 Behavior 10.5). A float of 10.0 is also passed to that reaction, which ignores it (Medium-High). Otherwise the hit deals the damage passed for this hit in full, straight to the hero's take-hit: it is not reduced by the hero's shield, armour, armour Mote or petpet factor (SPEC-0029 Behavior 10.5).

### Hit window

Each attack handler calls the shared routine only while the attack's animation progress is below 0.99. When progress reaches 0.99 the hit window closes. The progress test reads the animation's track data for that animation. If no track entry matches the animation, the animation counts as not finished, so the window stays open for the whole animation (High for the rule). "Progress" is the clip progress of SPEC-0005, (cursor + stored fraction) / frame count (High; Open question 15). The reach test itself has no phase check of its own.

Inside that window the boss predicate (event bits 0x1 to 0x10 on the current frame) decides when the shared hit can land. In the King's clips (fight region 1264, monster row 16, which has no weapon volumes) only bit 0x1 is used, on these frames (High):

| Animation | Used by | Frames with bit 0x1 on |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0x15 | lunge | 5 to 10 of 20 |
| 0x16 | swing (alternating with 0x17), roll counter-attack, first combo hit | 5 to 8 of 18 |

### Hit test and blocking (Confidence: High for the caller gates, dimensions, query order and first-record rule; Medium for the secondary sweep's exact geometry)

The reach test used by the shared hit runs in this order:

1. Distance gate. The full three-dimensional distance between the boss position and hero position must be at most 65.0 units. A greater distance fails before any body shape is tested.
2. Weapon points. Let A be the boss's weapon anchor and D1 and D2 the two pose-derived weapon directions. The start point is P0 = A − 1.5D1. The static weapon cylinder has radius 2.5, length 7.0, centre P0 and axis D1. Its second endpoint is E1 = P0 + 7.0D1; the alternative endpoint is E2 = P0 + 7.0D2.
3. Static test. For each positive-length hero body record in list order, first test the weapon cylinder at P0 against that record's finite cylinder (radius +0x44, length +0x40), using the static overlap of SPEC-0018.
4. Secondary sweep. Only after a static miss, compare E1 and E2. If their spatial components differ, call the shared secondary spatial helper using the hero record's radius and length and the prepared endpoint/frame inputs. If the endpoints are equal, skip this helper for that record. The helper is the swept capsule-against-triangle test of SPEC-0036 (Hit detection, swept step), with the triangle P0, E1, E2 and the hero record's capsule (radius from +0x44, length from +0x40, axis the record's posed Y axis); its steps, the 0.4 edge margin and its quirks are specified there and apply unchanged (Medium-High for the vertex assignment, High for the helper's identity). It has no time or frame-counter input.
5. Attack timing. The swing, overhead counter, lunge and combo handlers invoke this reach route on eligible attack-window ticks, before animation progress reaches 0.99. This is a spatial query within the existing attack window, not a separate timed sweep phase.

The test returns true on the first record that either test touches, and false otherwise.

The attack reach is therefore decided by the cylinder tests, not by the 65.0 gate alone: the gate only limits how far the hero can be at all.

### Test vectors for the reach gate

- Boss and hero 65.0 units apart: the gate passes (the test is "at most"); a hit depends on the shape tests.

## Roll and coin rules

- A roll is an integer from 0 to 100 inclusive, which is 101 values. "Roll less than c" succeeds with probability c / 101, not c / 100. A chance of 0 never succeeds. "Roll at most c" succeeds with probability (c + 1) / 101, so a chance of 100 always succeeds.
- A coin is an integer from 0 to 1 inclusive, so each value has probability 1/2.
- A uniform integer drawn between two bounds is inclusive on both ends. If the two bounds are equal, the result is that value.
- The random generator is the shared one, seeded once from the console clock (SPEC-0014).

## Edge cases and error handling

- **Phase regression (quirk).** The phase-2 test in the shipped game excludes only phase 2, not phase 3. A phase-3 boss whose health is at least 0.37 and below 0.75 re-enters phase 2 (phase-2 chances). Reproduce this. Whether health can rise during play is not established.
- **Flame waves are unreachable in phase 1.** The phase 1 flame-wave chance is 0, so no roll is less than it. The shipped values allow flame waves in phase 2 (50) and phase 3 (75) only.
- **Flame-wave counter stops at 601.** The counter adds one only while it is at most 600, so it stays at 601 once it has passed the limit. A failed check does not reset it, so while the gates pass a roll is made every tick.
- **Flame-wave tier outside 0 to 2.** The roll never passes, so no wave starts.
- **Flame-wave distance is strict.** A boss exactly 10 or exactly 40 units from the hero does not start a wave.
- **Roll counter-attack is certain.** The counter-attack test is "less than or equal" against a chance of 100, which every roll meets. Reproduce this: a hero who ends a roll within 5 units is always counter-attacked. The attack chances elsewhere use "less than".
- **Roll counter-attack leaves the once flag set.** The roll counter-attack does not clear the shared once flag. If an earlier attack's hit set the flag and no attack selector has cleared it since, the counter-attack animation plays and deals no damage. The next attack selector clears the flag. Reproduce this.
- **Hop back needs the boss to be not ready.** The ready flag is cleared only at the boomerang release, so in practice the hop back runs only while a boomerang is in flight. Reproduce this gate.
- **Hop timer quirk.** The hop timer adds one on each movement tick on which the state is free, not the flinch, and the hero is within 25 units (not above 25), whether or not the boss is close or ready, and it adds before it is compared. It is checked and cleared only when the hero is closer than 5 units and the boss is not ready (the comparison is the new value above 60). A boss that has spent a long time at mid-range therefore passes the 60-tick check at its first close, not-ready tick. Reproduce this.
- **Cooldowns run in any state.** The attack cooldown counters add one before the state gate is checked, so they run down while the boss is busy. The boomerang counter resets when the catch ends; the lunge and combo counters reset when their attack starts; the swing counter resets when the swing starts and again when the swing state ends (the roll counter-attack included); the flame counter resets when the volley ends. Block, overhead and roll have no counter.
- **Boomerang timeout keeps the counter.** A flight that times out does not reset the boomerang counter, so a new throw may follow at once when the counter is past its cooldown.
- **Boss idle while the boomerang flies.** Because the throw ends with its animation, the boss may start a new choice during the flight. The ready flag keeps the boomerang, flame wave, swing, lunge and combo choices closed until the catch or the timeout. Block and jump are closed the same way. Roll is not.
- **Big laugh is unreachable (High).** The big-laugh state runs only when its request word is 1. The word starts at 0 and is only ever cleared: no routine of the King (pre-check, choosers, the 21 state handlers, the reach-test body, boomerang flight and damage, flame waves, debug text) stores a non-zero value to it, and the controller record is passed to no other routine. The big-laugh chance is never loaded. Keep the state, but it never runs.
- **No flame wave while a boomerang is out.** The flame-wave gate is the ready flag, which is 0 from the boomerang release to the catch or timeout.
- **Unused tunables.** These values are stored and registered, but no code in the boot program reads them: the big laugh chance and the three exhausted-pant values. The block chance for phase 3 is registered twice; this is harmless.
- **Phase thresholds use whole percents.** The thresholds are stored as whole numbers and divided by 100 when compared with the health fraction.
- **Phase entries take one tick.** The phase entries set idle in the same tick, so there is no animation wait.
- **Flinch and phase checks.** A flinch can interrupt any state. The catch, triumph, phase and death checks of the same tick still run after the flinch.
- **Reaction trigger latch.** The trigger's rising edge is recorded even when the boss is busy, so a hero swing that starts while the boss is busy never triggers a block, jump or roll.
- **Shared hit happens once per attack.** After the first hit that passes the reach test, the same swing, overhead counter, lunge or combo animation deals nothing more, even if the hero stays in reach or the animation continues. A new attack starts with the flag clear (cleared at its start) and may hit again. A combo may hit once in each of its three animations.
- **Hits that miss do not use the flag.** A tick where the boss predicate or the reach test fails leaves the once flag clear, so a later tick in the same window can still hit.
- **Blocked hits use the flag.** A hero who is guarding and faces the boss takes no damage from the shared hit, for any attack. The hit still sets the once flag, so that attack cannot hit again. For example, a blocked overhead counter deals 0 (not 15) and sets the flag.
- **Feedback value has no effect.** On a blocked hit the hero's block reaction is given the float 10.0 along with the 0 damage, but the reaction does not read it (Medium-High). A clean build may drop it.
- **Phase speed (High).** The phase handlers store 1.2 and 1.4 in the controller's speed word, the same word the top of every controller pass copies into P (it starts at 1.0, and the two handlers are its only other writers). So the King plays at R = 1.0, 1.2 and 1.4 in phases 1, 2 and 3: clips advance 0.25 R key per tick and root travel scales with them (G15 gives the gate table). The earlier reading that P is a constant 1.0 was wrong. The scene clips the arena script plays on the King (actions 0x10, 0x11, 0x12 and 0x1B) are played while the controller is not running, and the shared update resets P to 1.0 on every tick, so they play at R = 1; the developer names of the two words (mouth wipe, crazy rage) are only names.
- **Hop back moves without a target.** The hop-back state sets no movement, but its clip's root motion would carry the boss 7.36 units backward over 60 ticks. In play the next tick's movement choice sees the hero closer than 10 and no orbit running, so it starts an orbit and the hop lasts one tick (Movement). A hop back also clears the hop timer and consumes one roll, which is its only lasting effect.
- **Jump chooser runs every tick.** The movement choice calls the same jump chooser as the reaction edge, so a hero who comes within 5 units of a King that is more than 15 units from home triggers a jump at once, with probability c / 101 per tick (c from 5 at 15 units rising to 80 at 28 units and on). Reproduce this: the King returns home when the hero closes in on it.
- **Phase entries hold the controller.** Both phase entries set base state 33 in the pre-check. In pack 1246 the arena script releases the King at the end of its phase scene; with no script run (the hero outside the trigger sphere) the King stays frozen and unhittable. A rewrite that omits the scenes still has to hold the King for the scene's length (Fight AI build guide, G10).
- **Cooldown counters stop.** Each cooldown counter stops rising at its cooldown plus one (481 or 601); a long wait does not lengthen the first roll.
- **Orbit never ends by itself.** An orbit at a hero distance of 10 to 25 units runs until one of the end conditions in Movement occurs.
